#  Chapter 01 — Network Fundamentals

> [!abstract] Chapter Goal
> Build the working vocabulary you will need for the rest of the vault: what a network *is*, what lives inside it, how the pieces are wired together, and the high-level patterns (topologies, modes, architectures) that recur at every later layer.

This chapter is intentionally light on protocol specifics — those come in [[02 - Reference Models (OSI & TCP-IP)|Chapter 2]] onward. The aim here is to make sure that when you read "the switch floods the broadcast frame because no CAM entry exists", you immediately know *what a switch is*, *what a broadcast is*, and *why flooding is even a thing*.

##  Learning Outcomes

After this chapter you should be able to:

- Define a computer network in one sentence and list four objectives it serves.
- Classify any device as either end (DTE) or intermediate, and place it correctly in the OSI stack.
- Choose an appropriate transmission medium given distance, bandwidth, and environmental constraints.
- Match a real-world scenario to its correct scale classification (LAN vs MAN vs WAN).
- Draw the five canonical topologies and explain the failure modes of each.
- Distinguish physical topology from logical topology with a concrete example.
- Given a protocol description, identify whether it is connection-oriented or connectionless, and whether it uses unicast, multicast, or broadcast.
- Compare client/server and P2P architectures along the axes of scalability, manageability, and security.
