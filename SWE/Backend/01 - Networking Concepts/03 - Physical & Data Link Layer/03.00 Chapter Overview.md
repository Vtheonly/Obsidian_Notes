#  Chapter 03 — Physical & Data Link Layer

> [!abstract] Chapter Goal
> Drill into how bits actually travel over copper, fiber, and radio — and how those bits are grouped into Ethernet frames. By the end you'll be able to decode a raw hex frame dump, classify any MAC as unicast/multicast/broadcast, and explain why a switch is fundamentally smarter than a hub.

This chapter merges the bottom two OSI layers (L1 Physical and L2 Data Link). These are tightly coupled in practice — you can't design one without considering the other — and most modern NICs and switches handle both.

##  Learning Outcomes

After this chapter you should be able to:

- Explain the LLC/MAC split and why IEEE 802 chose it.
- Given a MAC address, classify it as unicast/multicast/broadcast and identify the OUI.
- Decode a raw hex Ethernet frame into its constituent fields.
- Calculate the minimum Ethernet frame size and explain why padding exists.
- Compare hubs and switches across collision domains, broadcast domains, and throughput.
- Read the Cisco switch port LED colors and know what each STP state means.
- Distinguish the CAM table (switch) from the ARP cache (host/router) by purpose, layer, and CLI command.
