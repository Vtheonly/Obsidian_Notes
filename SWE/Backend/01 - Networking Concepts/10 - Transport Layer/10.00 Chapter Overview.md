#  Chapter 10 — Transport Layer

> [!abstract] Chapter Goal
> Move from host-to-host delivery (Layer 3) to **process-to-process delivery** (Layer 4). The transport layer is where ports and sockets live, where TCP builds reliable streams and UDP stays best-effort, and where flow/congestion control happen. Everything above — HTTP, DNS, email — sits on top of TCP or UDP.

##  Learning Outcomes

After this chapter you should be able to:

- Explain the difference between host-to-host (L3) and process-to-process (L4) delivery.
- Define a socket and a 5-tuple.
- List the well-known ports for HTTP, HTTPS, DNS, SSH, SMTP, etc. from memory.
- Trace the server-side socket lifecycle from `socket()` to `accept()`.
- Describe every field in the TCP header, including all 6 control flags.
- Walk through the TCP 3-way handshake and the 4-step close.
- Explain how TCP flow control (sliding window) differs from congestion control (cwnd).
- Diagnose whether a connection problem is at the transport layer or below.
- Choose between TCP and UDP for a new application.
