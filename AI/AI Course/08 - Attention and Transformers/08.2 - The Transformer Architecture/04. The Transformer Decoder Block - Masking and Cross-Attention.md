# 04. The Transformer Decoder Block — Masking and Cross-Attention

> **Prerequisites:** [[03. The Transformer Encoder Block]]
> **Connections:** [[02. Teacher Forcing and Exposure Bias]] (why the decoder is autoregressive), [[01. The Encoder-Decoder Pattern - Conditional Generation]] (the pattern being implemented)

## What the decoder does that the encoder does not

The encoder block from the previous note is symmetric: every position attends to every other position. This is fine for understanding (BERT-style tasks: classification, entity recognition, reading comprehension), where the whole input is available at once.

The decoder is different in two ways:

1. **It generates autoregressively.** At inference time, it produces one token at a time, and each token can only attend to *previously generated* tokens — not future ones. This is enforced by **causal masking** in self-attention.

2. **It attends to the encoder's output.** In an Encoder-Decoder Transformer (for translation, summarization), the decoder needs to look at the input. This is **cross-attention** — a second attention operation that attends from decoder positions to encoder positions.

These two differences mean the decoder block has **three** sublayers, not two.

## Sublayer 1: Masked self-attention

This is standard self-attention with one modification: **a mask is applied to the attention matrix to prevent positions from attending to future positions.**

The unmasked attention matrix is $Q K^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$. Entry $(i, j)$ is the score for query $i$ attending to key $j$. For causal (autoregressive) generation, position $i$ should only attend to positions $j \leq i$ — the future positions $j > i$ are not yet generated.

The mask sets the scores for $j > i$ to $-\infty$ before the softmax:

$$\text{MaskedScores}_{i,j} = \begin{cases} (Q K^\top)_{i,j} & \text{if } j \leq i \\ -\infty & \text{if } j > i \end{cases}$$

After softmax, $e^{-\infty} = 0$, so the attention weights for future positions are exactly zero. Position $i$ attends only to positions $1, \ldots, i$.

This is what makes the decoder **autoregressive**: at inference time, you generate token 1 from just token 1's query (attending to itself and the encoder), then token 2 from tokens 1–2, then token 3 from tokens 1–3, and so on. Each token can only see the past, never the future.

> [!IMPORTANT] The mask is triangular.
> The causal mask is an upper-triangular matrix of $-\infty$ values. This is the "look-ahead mask" — it prevents looking ahead. Visually, the attention matrix becomes lower-triangular: the diagonal and below are real scores, above the diagonal is zero.

### Why masking matters for training

During training, the decoder receives all target tokens at once (teacher forcing — see [[02. Teacher Forcing and Exposure Bias]]). Without the mask, position 5 could attend to position 10, "cheating" by looking at the answer. The mask prevents this, ensuring that the training loss at each position reflects a genuine prediction task (predict token $t$ from tokens $1, \ldots, t-1$ and the encoder output), not a copy task.

The mask is what lets us train the decoder in parallel (all positions at once) while preserving the autoregressive property (each position only sees the past). Without it, you would have to train the decoder sequentially, one position at a time, which would forfeit the Transformer's parallelism advantage.

## Sublayer 2: Cross-attention

This is the second attention operation, and it is where the decoder "looks at" the encoder's output. The query comes from the decoder; the keys and values come from the encoder:

$$Q_{dec} = Y W_Q^{cross}, \quad K_{enc} = X_{enc} W_K^{cross}, \quad V_{enc} = X_{enc} W_V^{cross}$$
$$\text{CrossAttention} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_{dec} K_{enc}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V_{enc}$$

The attention matrix here is $T_{dec} \times T_{enc}$ — each decoder position attends to all encoder positions. This is the direct descendant of Bahdanau attention from Chapter 07, now reformulated as dot-product attention.

Cross-attention is what makes this an **Encoder-Decoder** Transformer rather than a decoder-only one. Decoder-only models (GPT) omit this sublayer — they have no encoder to attend to. We cover this design choice in [[01. The Encoder-Decoder Design Space - BERT vs GPT vs T5]].

---

## Sublayer 3: Feedforward

Same as the encoder's FFN — a position-wise MLP providing nonlinearity. No new ideas here.

## How the decoder generates at inference time

At inference, the decoder generates one token at a time:

1. **Step 1:** Feed `<BOS>` (beginning of sequence) as the only input. The decoder produces a distribution over the first output token. Sample (or argmax) token $y_1$.
2. **Step 2:** Feed `<BOS>, y_1` as input. The decoder produces a distribution over the second token. Sample $y_2$.
3. **Step 3:** Feed `<BOS>, y_1, y_2`. Sample $y_3$.
4. ...continue until `<EOS>` is generated.

At each step, the **masked self-attention** ensures the new token's query can only attend to previously generated tokens, and the **cross-attention** lets it attend to the encoder's output (which is computed once and reused).

> [!NOTE] KV caching.
> Naively, step $t$ recomputes attention over all $t$ previous tokens, making generation $O(T^2)$ total. In practice, **KV caching** stores the keys and values from previous steps and only computes the new token's Q, K, V, reducing per-step cost to $O(T)$. This is an engineering optimization, not an architectural change — the math is the same.

## Encoder-decoder vs decoder-only: a preview

The decoder block we have built has cross-attention, which makes it suitable for sequence-to-sequence tasks (translation, summarization) where there is a distinct input and output. But many modern models (GPT, LLaMA) are **decoder-only**: they omit cross-attention and use only masked self-attention + FFN. These models are trained on a single sequence (e.g., "predict the next token in this text"), with no separate encoder.

The choice between encoder-decoder and decoder-only is the subject of [[01. The Encoder-Decoder Design Space - BERT vs GPT vs T5]]. The short version: encoder-decoder is natural for tasks with distinct inputs and outputs; decoder-only is simpler and dominates modern LLMs because it can be trained on any text without needing input/output pairs.

---

## What you should walk away with

1. The decoder block has three sublayers: masked self-attention, cross-attention, FFN — each wrapped in residual + LayerNorm.
2. **Masked self-attention** prevents positions from attending to the future, which is what makes the decoder autoregressive. The mask is an upper-triangular $-\infty$ matrix.
3. **Cross-attention** lets the decoder attend to the encoder's output — this is Bahdanau attention reformulated as dot-product attention.
4. The mask is what lets the decoder train in parallel (all positions at once) while preserving the autoregressive property.
5. Decoder-only models (GPT) omit cross-attention and use only masked self-attention + FFN.
