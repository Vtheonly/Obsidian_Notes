# 2. Key Papers and References

> A curated list of the papers, datasets, tools, and resources most relevant to the chess commentary verification project. Each entry includes the citation, a one-paragraph summary, and a note on how it relates to this project.

---

## 2.1 Chess Commentary Generation

### Learning to Generate Move-by-Move Commentary for Chess Games from Large-Scale Social Forum Data (ACL 2018)

- **Authors**: Harsh Jhamtani, Varun Gangal, Eduard Hovy, Graham Neubig, Taylor Berg-Kirkpatrick
- **Link**: [ACL Anthology P18-1154](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1154/)
- **Year**: 2018

**Summary**: Introduces a dataset of ~298,000 (move, commentary) pairs collected from online chess forums. Trains neural models to generate commentary from (board state, move) pairs.

**Relation to our project**: The dataset is reusable for our project (we can repurpose it as a verification dataset). The methodology (per-move alignment, claim type categorization) informs our design. The direction is opposite (board → commentary vs. commentary → verdict).

---

### Improving Chess Commentaries by Combining Language Models with Symbolic Reasoning Engines (2022)

- **Link**: [arXiv 2212.08195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08195)
- **Year**: 2022

**Summary**: Combines Stockfish, a symbolic chess engine, and a language model to generate grounded commentary. The LLM only converts structured facts into English; it does not reason about chess.

**Relation to our project**: The closest existing work. Same architectural pattern (separate language from reasoning), opposite direction. We reuse their fact vocabulary as our claim type taxonomy. See [[Chapter 5. Research Landscape/2. Hybrid Symbolic and Language Models|2. Hybrid Symbolic and Language Models]].

---

### Automated Chess Commentator Powered by Neural Chess Engine (2019)

- **Link**: [arXiv 1909.10413](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10413)
- **Year**: 2019

**Summary**: Uses Leela Chess Zero (neural chess engine) to generate categorized commentary (move description, planning, comparison, evaluation).

**Relation to our project**: Their commentary categorization is the basis for our claim type taxonomy. See [[Chapter 5. Research Landscape/3. Neural Chess Commentary Systems|3. Neural Chess Commentary Systems]].

## 2.2 NLP and LLMs in Chess

### SentiMATE: Learning to play Chess through Natural Language Processing (2019)

- **Link**: [arXiv 1907.08321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08321)
- **Year**: 2019

**Summary**: Uses chess commentary as supervision for learning move quality. Validates that commentary contains structured information that can be learned from.

**Relation to our project**: Validates that commentary is a useful signal. Our project assumes the same: commentary makes factual claims that can be extracted and verified. See [[Chapter 5. Research Landscape/5. NLP Applied to Chess|5. NLP Applied to Chess]].

---

### Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking (AAAI 2023)

- **Link**: [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/21390)
- **Year**: 2023

**Summary**: Tests whether LLMs can track board state across a sequence of moves. Finds that small models lose track after ~10 plies; large models degrade after ~15-20 plies; even large models cannot reliably count material.

**Relation to our project**: Empirical evidence that LLMs cannot reliably track board state, justifying our architectural separation. Cite this when defending the design. See [[Chapter 5. Research Landscape/5. NLP Applied to Chess|5. NLP Applied to Chess]].

---

### ChessQA: Evaluating Large Language Models for Chess Understanding

- **Link**: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397006403_ChessQA_Evaluating_Large_Language_Models_for_Chess_Understanding)

**Summary**: A benchmark of chess puzzles (FEN + best move) evaluating LLM chess reasoning. GPT-4 solves ~30% of easy puzzles, ~10% of medium, ~3% of hard. Engines solve ~100%.

**Relation to our project**: Direct evidence that LLMs are bad at tactical reasoning. Our verifier uses the symbolic layer (python-chess attack maps) for tactical claims. See [[Chapter 5. Research Landscape/5. NLP Applied to Chess|5. NLP Applied to Chess]].

## 2.3 Datasets

### ACL Chess Commentary dataset

- **Size**: ~298,000 (move, commentary) pairs.
- **Source**: Reddit r/chess and other forums.
- **Link**: [ACL Anthology P18-1154](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1154/)

**Use for our project**: Primary source for the CCVB benchmark. Sample 1200 examples (60% of CCVB).

---

### Synthetic Chess Commentary Dataset (Kaggle)

- **Size**: ~50,000 positions with commentary.
- **Source**: Lichess games + Stockfish + Gemini.
- **Link**: [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jayanthrajg/chess-commentary-dataset)

**Use for our project**: Secondary source for CCVB. LLM-generated, so contains hallucinations — useful for testing the verifier on likely-incorrect commentary. Sample 600 examples (30% of CCVB).

---

### Massive PGN datasets (Hugging Face)

- **Size**: Millions of games, billions of positions.
- **Source**: Lichess, Chess.com, professional databases.
- **Link**: [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/InterwebAlchemy/pgn-dataset)

**Use for our project**: Not directly useful for verification (no commentary), but useful for building a large facts database cache and stress-testing python-chess.

---

### Lichess Elite database

- **Size**: ~100 million games.
- **Source**: Lichess, filtered to 2200+ Elo.
- **Link**: [Lichess database](https://database.lichess.org/)

**Use for our project**: Same as above. High-quality games for facts database testing.

## 2.8 Reading Order for Newcomers

If you are new to the project, read in this order:

1. The ACL 2018 paper (commentary generation, foundational).
2. The 2022 hybrid paper (closest existing work, architectural inspiration).
3. The AAAI 2023 state-tracking paper (empirical evidence for LLM limitations).
4. The python-chess documentation (core dependency).
5. The Stockfish UCI documentation (core dependency).
6. This vault, in order from Chapter 1 to Chapter 10.

After reading these, you should have enough context to start contributing to the project.
