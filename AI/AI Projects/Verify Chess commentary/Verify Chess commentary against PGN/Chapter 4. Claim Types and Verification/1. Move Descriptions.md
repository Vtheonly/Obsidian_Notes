# 1. Move Descriptions

> **A move description in one sentence**: A claim that a specific piece moved to a specific square, optionally specifying the color, the move type, or special properties (check, capture, promotion).

This is the most common claim type in chess commentary, the easiest to verify, and the one with the highest hit rate for LLM hallucinations. Get this right and you have caught roughly half of all errors.

---

## 1.1 The Claim Schema

```json
{
  "claim_type": "move_description",
  "params": {
    "color": "white | black",
    "piece": "pawn | knight | bishop | rook | queen | king",
    "destination_square": "e5",
    "is_check": false,
    "is_capture": false,
    "is_castling": false,
    "is_promotion": false,
    "promotion_piece": null
  },
  "target_ply_hint": null
}
```

All fields except `color` and `piece` are optional. A minimal claim is just "color + piece moved somewhere"; the verifier searches for any matching ply.

---

## 1.2 What the LLM Extracts

Given an English sentence, the LLM identifies:

- The piece type and color (or `null` if implicit from context).
- The destination square (or `null` if not stated).
- Special flags (check, capture, castling, promotion) if the sentence mentions them.

### Example extractions

| English | Extracted params |
|---|---|
| "The knight jumps to e5." | `{color: null, piece: "knight", destination: "e5"}` |
| "White castles kingside." | `{color: "white", piece: "king", is_castling: true}` |
| "Black promotes to a queen." | `{color: "black", piece: "pawn", is_promotion: true, promotion_piece: "queen"}` |
| "The bishop gives check on b4." | `{color: null, piece: "bishop", destination: "b4", is_check: true}` |

> [!info] Implicit color
> When the color is not stated ("The knight jumps to e5"), the LLM should set `color: null`. The verifier then searches both colors and emits `Ambiguous` if both have a matching move. This is correct behavior — the author should specify the color.

---

## 1.3 Verification Rule

The verifier's job is to find a ply in the facts database that matches **every** non-null field in the claim.

### 1.3.1 Algorithm

```text
1. Collect all plies where:
   - claim.color is null OR ply.mover == claim.color
   - ply.piece_type == piece_code(claim.piece)
   - claim.destination_square is null OR ply.to_square == square_index(claim.destination_square)
   - claim.is_check is null OR ply.is_check == claim.is_check
   - claim.is_capture is null OR ply.is_capture == claim.is_capture
   - claim.is_castling is null OR ply.is_castling == claim.is_castling
   - claim.is_promotion is null OR ply.is_promotion == claim.is_promotion
   - claim.promotion_piece is null OR ply.promotion_piece == piece_code(claim.promotion_piece)
2. If exactly one ply matches: emit "Verified" with that ply's SAN.
3. If zero plies match: emit "Possible hallucination" with a list of close misses.
4. If multiple plies match: emit "Ambiguous" with the list of matching ply indices.
```

### 1.3.2 Close misses

When emitting `Possible hallucination`, the verifier should explain what was close. Examples:

- "No knight move to e5 was found. However, a knight moved to e4 at ply 14 and a bishop moved to e5 at ply 16. Did you mean one of these?"
- "No white castling move was found. White's king is still on e1 at the end of the game."

Close misses are the verifier's most useful feature for authors — they often reveal the actual error (typo in the square, wrong piece type).

## 1.4 Worked Examples

### Example 1: Clean verified

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination: "f3"}`
**Facts**: Ply 2 is `Nf3` for White.
**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 2 is Nf3, a white knight moving to f3."

### Example 2: Wrong piece type

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination: "f3"}`
**Facts**: Ply 2 is `Bf3` for White (a bishop moved to f3).
**Verdict**: Contradicted. "No white knight move to f3 was found. A white bishop moved to f3 at ply 2 (Bf3). Did you mean bishop?"

### Example 3: Wrong square

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination: "e5"}`
**Facts**: Ply 14 is `Ne4` for White.
**Verdict**: Contradicted. "No white knight move to e5 was found. A white knight moved to e4 at ply 14 (Ne4). Did you mean e4?"

### Example 4: Ambiguous (multiple matches)

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination: "f3"}` with no hint.
**Facts**: Plies 2 and 22 are both `Nf3` for White (the knight went there, came back, went there again).
**Verdict**: Ambiguous. "Multiple white knight moves to f3 found at plies 2 and 22. Provide a target_ply_hint or specify additional constraints."

### Example 5: Implicit color (no match)

**Claim**: `{color: null, piece: "knight", destination: "e5"}`
**Facts**: No knight of either color moved to e5.
**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "No knight move to e5 was found in the game."

### Example 6: Implicit color (ambiguous)

**Claim**: `{color: null, piece: "knight", destination: "f3"}`
**Facts**: Ply 2 is `Nf3` for White; ply 19 is `Nf3` for Black.
**Verdict**: Ambiguous. "Both colors have a knight move to f3 (White at ply 2, Black at ply 19). Specify the color."

## 1.5 Special Cases

### 1.5.1 Castling

"The king castles kingside" is a move description claim with `is_castling: true`. python-chess represents castling as a king move (e.g., `O-O` is `e1g1`), and `board.is_castling(move)` returns `True`. The verifier checks the flag, not the destination square (the destination square is the king's post-castling square, which the author rarely specifies).

### 1.5.2 En passant

"The pawn captures en passant on e6" is a capture claim with an implicit en-passant flag. python-chess exposes `board.is_en_passant(move)`. If the claim does not specify e.p., the verifier treats it as a normal capture — but if the actual move was e.p., the explanation should mention that.

### 1.5.3 Promotion

"The pawn promotes to a queen" sets `is_promotion: true` and `promotion_piece: "queen"`. python-chess represents promotion as a pawn move with a `promotion` field on the `Move` object. The verifier checks both.

### 1.5.4 Check and checkmate

"The bishop gives check" sets `is_check: true`. The verifier cross-references `ply.is_check` from the facts database. "The bishop checkmates" sets both `is_check: true` and (separately) is a checkmate claim; see Chapter 4.3 for tactical claims.

### 1.5.5 "The king moves to g8"

A king move description is verified exactly like any other piece. python-chess tracks king moves like any other piece. No special handling needed.

## 1.6 Common LLM Mistakes

The LLM makes specific recurring mistakes on move descriptions. Knowing them helps you design the prompt and the validation pipeline.

### 1.6.1 Confusing "moves to" with "captures on"

"The bishop moves to h7" should be `move_description` with `is_capture: false`. The LLM sometimes sets `is_capture: true` because h7 happens to have a piece. The verifier then flags the claim as `Contradicted` (the actual move `Bxh7` was a capture, contradicting the `is_capture: false` field). This is the verifier working correctly; the LLM made the mistake.

### 1.6.2 Omitting the color

"The knight jumps to e5" — the LLM often leaves color as `null`. This is correct behavior. The verifier then disambiguates or marks `Ambiguous`. Do not "fix" this by having the LLM guess the color; the verifier is the right place for disambiguation.

### 1.6.3 Misidentifying the piece type

"The horse jumps to e5" — the LLM (especially older or smaller models) sometimes says "horse" instead of "knight". The prompt should include a vocabulary section mapping informal terms to formal piece types.

### 1.6.4 Misreading the square

"The knight moves to e5" when the sentence was actually "The knight moves to e4". This is an LLM transcription error. The fix is to always include `source_text` in the claim so the verifier can compare against the verbatim quote.

---

## 1.7 Tips for Authors

When writing chess notes that this system will verify, the following tips will reduce false positives:

- **Always specify the color** when ambiguous: "White's knight" rather than "the knight".
- **Use the SAN square notation**: "e5", not "the fifth square of the e-file".
- **Mention special moves explicitly**: "castles kingside", "promotes to a queen", "captures en passant".
- **Avoid pronouns when possible**: "The bishop captures the rook" is better than "It captures the rook".
- **One move per sentence**: "The knight moves to e5, then the bishop pins the rook" is two claims in one sentence; split them.

---

## 1.8 Key Reminders

- Move descriptions are the most common claim type and the easiest to verify.
- Schema: color, piece, destination, plus optional flags (check, capture, castling, promotion).
- Verification rule: find a ply matching every non-null field. Exactly one match → Verified. Zero → Possible hallucination with close misses. Multiple → Ambiguous.
- Always include `source_text` in the claim so the verifier can validate against the verbatim quote.
- Castling, en passant, and promotion have special handling but fit the same schema.
- LLM mistakes: confusing "moves to" with "captures on", omitting color, misidentifying piece type, misreading squares. The prompt and validation pipeline should defend against each.
- Author tips: specify color, use SAN squares, mention special moves explicitly, avoid pronouns, one move per sentence.
- Move descriptions catch roughly half of all LLM hallucinations. Get this right first.
