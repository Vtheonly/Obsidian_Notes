# 5. Material Claims

> **A material claim in one sentence**: A claim about the material balance — "White wins a rook", "Black is up a pawn", "Material is equal" — that the verifier checks by comparing piece counts before and after the relevant plies.

Material claims are the third most common type and the easiest to verify after move descriptions. They are pure counting: compare the piece lists before and after, compute the difference, compare to the claim.

---

## 5.1 The Claim Schema

```json
{
  "claim_type": "material_gain | material_loss | material_equal",
  "params": {
    "gaining_color": "white | black | null",
    "piece_won": "pawn | knight | bishop | rook | queen | null",
    "count": 1,
    "window": "single_ply | move_sequence | game_total"
  },
  "target_ply_hint": null,
  "window_end_ply_hint": null
}
```

Three subtypes:

- **`material_gain`**: one side wins material.
- **`material_loss`**: one side loses material (the opposite of gain; included for symmetry).
- **`material_equal`**: material is equal at the relevant ply.

The `window` field tells the verifier how to count:

- `single_ply`: compare material before and after one ply.
- `move_sequence`: compare material at the start and end of a sequence of plies.
- `game_total`: compare starting material to material at the end of the game.

---

## 5.2 Verification Rule

### 5.2.1 Single-ply material gain

```text
1. Find the ply (using target_ply_hint or by searching for any capture
   by gaining_color).
2. Compute material_before = total_material(gaining_color, ply - 1).
3. Compute material_after = total_material(gaining_color, ply).
4. delta = material_after - material_before.
5. Check delta against claim.count * piece_value(claim.piece_won).
```

`piece_value`: pawn = 1, knight = 3, bishop = 3, rook = 5, queen = 9.

### 5.2.2 Move-sequence material gain

```text
1. Identify the start and end plies of the sequence.
   - Start: target_ply_hint (or the first ply of the game if null).
   - End: window_end_ply_hint (or the last ply of the game if null).
2. Compute material at start and end for gaining_color.
3. delta = material_end - material_start.
4. Check delta against claim.count * piece_value(claim.piece_won).
```

### 5.2.3 Game-total material gain

```text
1. material_start = 39 for both colors (8 pawns + 2 knights + 2 bishops
   + 2 rooks + 1 queen = 8 + 6 + 6 + 10 + 9 = 39).
2. material_end = total_material(gaining_color, last_ply).
3. delta = material_end - material_start.
4. Check delta against claim.count * piece_value(claim.piece_won).
```

### 5.2.4 Material-equal claims

```text
1. Find the relevant ply (or use the last ply of the game if no hint).
2. Compute material for both colors.
3. If equal: Verified.
4. If not equal: Contradicted with the actual delta.
```

## 5.3 Worked Examples

### Example 1: Single-ply pawn win

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "pawn", count: 1, window: "single_ply"`, target_ply_hint: 12
**Facts**: Ply 12 is `Bxh7+` (white bishop captures black pawn).
- material_before (ply 11): white=39, black=39
- material_after (ply 12): white=39, black=38
- delta for white: 0 (white did not gain anything; white captured but the captured pawn is not added to white's material)

Wait — this is a subtle point. "White wins a pawn" usually means White has one more pawn than before, not that White captured a pawn. But capturing a pawn reduces Black's material, which changes the **delta** (white - black) without changing White's absolute material.

Two interpretations:
- **Interpretation A**: "White wins a pawn" means White's pawn count increases by 1. This requires White to capture a pawn AND promote (impossible in one ply) or to capture a pawn that was previously captured by Black (also impossible in one ply). Effectively, single-ply material gain is impossible under this interpretation.
- **Interpretation B**: "White wins a pawn" means the material balance shifts in White's favor by 1 pawn. White's absolute count stays the same; Black's decreases by 1.

The verifier uses **Interpretation B**. The "delta" is `material_balance(gaining_color) - material_balance(other_color)`, where `material_balance(color)` is the total material for that color.

So for the example:

- material_balance(white) at ply 11: 39
- material_balance(black) at ply 11: 39
- delta before: 0
- material_balance(white) at ply 12: 39 (white captured but the bishop replaces — wait, no, white captured with the bishop, the bishop remains)

OK let me redo. Material balance is the sum of piece values still on the board for each color.

- Ply 11: White has 8 pawns + 2 knights + 2 bishops + 2 rooks + 1 queen = 39. Black has the same = 39. Delta = 0.
- Ply 12 (Bxh7+): White still has all 39 (the bishop moved but is still on the board). Black has 7 pawns (one was captured) + everything else = 38. Delta = +1 for White.

So under Interpretation B, "White wins a pawn" at ply 12 is `Verified`. The delta is +1 (one pawn's worth of material).

**Verdict**: Verified. "At ply 12, White captured a black pawn (Bxh7+). Material balance shifts from 0 to +1 for White. The claim that White wins a pawn is verified."

### Example 2: Move-sequence rook win

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "rook", count: 1, window: "move_sequence"`, target_ply_hint: 12, window_end_ply_hint: 24
**Facts**: At ply 12, material balance is 0. At ply 24, material balance is +5 for White (White has captured a rook without losing anything of equal value in between).
- delta = +5
- claim.count * piece_value(rook) = 1 * 5 = 5
- Match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "Between plies 12 and 24, the material balance shifts from 0 to +5 for White. The claim that White wins a rook in this sequence is verified."

### Example 3: Hallucinated material gain

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "rook", count: 1, window: "move_sequence"`, target_ply_hint: 12, window_end_ply_hint: 24
**Facts**: At ply 12, material balance is 0. At ply 24, material balance is still 0 (no material was won or lost).
- delta = 0
- claim expects +5
- Mismatch.

**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "Between plies 12 and 24, material remains equal. No material gain occurred. The claim that White wins a rook is not supported."

### Example 4: Material equal

**Claim**: `material_equal` with target_ply_hint: 20
**Facts**: At ply 20, material balance is 0.
**Verdict**: Verified. "At ply 20, material is equal (39 vs 39)."

### Example 5: Material equal but claim is wrong

**Claim**: `material_equal` with target_ply_hint: 20
**Facts**: At ply 20, material balance is +2 for White (White won a pawn at ply 12).
**Verdict**: Contradicted. "At ply 20, material is +2 for White (White has one extra pawn). The claim that material is equal is contradicted."

### Example 6: Partial credit (gained less than claimed)

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "rook", count: 1, window: "move_sequence"`, target_ply_hint: 12, window_end_ply_hint: 24
**Facts**: At ply 12, balance is 0. At ply 24, balance is +3 for White (White won a knight, not a rook).
- delta = +3
- claim expects +5
- Mismatch.

**Verdict**: Contradicted. "Between plies 12 and 24, material balance shifts from 0 to +3 for White. White gained a knight (or equivalent), not a rook. The claim that White wins a rook is contradicted; White wins approximately a knight."

## 5.4 Subtle Cases

### 5.4.1 Winning material then losing it

If White wins a pawn at ply 12 and loses a pawn at ply 18, the game-total delta is 0 but the single-ply delta at ply 12 is +1. The claim "White wins a pawn at ply 12" is `Verified`; the claim "White wins a pawn in the game" is `Contradicted` (the net result is 0).

The verifier must be careful about the `window`. Always check what window the claim specifies.

### 5.4.2 Exchange (winning the exchange)

"White wins the exchange" means White wins a rook for a minor piece (knight or bishop), netting +2 (5 - 3 = 2). The verifier should recognize this pattern:

- delta = +2 for the gaining color
- the gaining color captured a rook
- the gaining color lost a knight or bishop (in the same sequence)

If the claim is "White wins the exchange", the verifier checks for this pattern.

### 5.4.3 Promotion

A pawn promotion changes the material balance significantly (a pawn becomes a queen, +8). The verifier must count the promoted piece as a queen, not a pawn, after the promotion ply.

### 5.4.4 Capturing the queen

Capturing the queen is +9, often decisive. The verifier should highlight this in the explanation: "Material balance shifts by +9 (queen capture)".

### 5.4.5 Piece values for bishops and knights

Bishops and knights are both valued at 3 in the default scheme. Some authors use 3.25 for bishops (the "bishop pair" advantage). The verifier uses 3 for both; if the claim is "White wins a bishop for a knight", that is a +0 delta and the claim is technically about even-material — the verifier should mark it `Ambiguous` because the bishop-vs-knight value is debatable.

## 5.5 The Material Timeline

The facts database stores a `material_timeline` array (see Chapter 2.4.4) that gives the material balance at every ply. This makes material claim verification O(1) — no need to recount pieces, just look up the timeline.

```python
def verify_material_gain(claim, facts):
    start_ply = claim.target_ply_hint or 0
    end_ply = claim.window_end_ply_hint or (len(facts.plies) - 1)
    
    delta = (facts.material_timeline[end_ply].delta
             - facts.material_timeline[start_ply].delta)
    
    expected = piece_value(claim.piece_won) * claim.count
    
    if claim.gaining_color == "black":
        delta = -delta  # delta is from White's perspective
    
    if delta == expected:
        return Verdict("Verified", f"Material delta = +{delta} for {claim.gaining_color}.")
    elif delta > 0 and delta < expected:
        return Verdict("Contradicted",
                       f"Material delta = +{delta}, expected +{expected}. "
                       f"Partial gain; less than claimed.")
    elif delta == 0:
        return Verdict("Possible hallucination",
                       "No material change occurred.")
    else:
        return Verdict("Contradicted",
                       f"Material delta = {delta}, expected +{expected}.")
```

## 5.6 Common LLM Mistakes

### 5.6.1 Confusing capture with material gain

"White captures a pawn" is a capture claim, not a material claim. The LLM sometimes tags it as `material_gain`. The validator should reject: a single capture with no sequence context should be a capture claim.

### 5.6.2 Wrong window

"White wins a rook" without specifying the window is ambiguous. The LLM should default to `move_sequence` with the start ply being the first capture in the sequence and the end ply being the last ply of the game (or the next major material change). The verifier should mark claims with ambiguous windows as `Ambiguous`.

### 5.6.3 Inverting the gaining color

"White wins a rook" should set `gaining_color: "white"`. The LLM sometimes inverts this, especially when the sentence is in passive voice ("a rook is won by Black"). The validator should sanity-check: if the sentence says "White wins", `gaining_color` must be `"white"`.

### 5.6.4 Wrong piece type

"White wins a rook" when the actual gain was a knight. The verifier flags this as `Contradicted` with the actual delta and the actual piece captured.

---

## 5.7 Tips for Authors

- **Specify the window**: "in this sequence", "in the game", "with this move".
- **Specify the piece**: "wins a rook" is clearer than "wins material".
- **Distinguish gain from exchange**: "wins a pawn" is gain; "wins the exchange" is a specific trade pattern.
- **Use material-equal claims sparingly**: "material is equal" is a strong claim that the verifier checks strictly.

---

## 5.8 Key Reminders

- Material claims are pure counting: compare material balance before and after.
- Three subtypes: gain, loss, equal. Three windows: single-ply, move-sequence, game-total.
- Material balance is from White's perspective: positive = White is up.
- "Winning material" means the material balance shifts in your favor, not that your absolute count increases (capturing reduces the opponent's count).
- The material_timeline in the facts database makes verification O(1).
- Exchange (rook for minor piece) = +2 delta. Queen capture = +9. Pawn = +1.
- Promotion changes the count: a promoted queen is +8.
- Common LLM mistakes: confusing capture with material gain, wrong window, inverting gaining color, wrong piece type.
- Author tips: specify window, specify piece, distinguish gain from exchange, use material-equal claims sparingly.
- Material claims are the third most common type and very high yield for hallucination detection.
