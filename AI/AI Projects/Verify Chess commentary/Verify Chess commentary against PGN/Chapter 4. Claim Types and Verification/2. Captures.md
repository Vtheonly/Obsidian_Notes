# 2. Captures

> **A capture claim in one sentence**: A claim that one piece captured another, optionally specifying the capturing piece, the captured piece, the colors, the square, and whether it was a sacrifice.

Capture claims are the second most common type and the second most hallucination-prone. They are slightly harder to verify than move descriptions because they involve two pieces (the capturer and the captured) and often a sacrifice judgment.

---

## 2.1 The Claim Schema

```json
{
  "claim_type": "capture",
  "params": {
    "color": "white | black | null",
    "piece": "pawn | knight | ... | null",
    "target_color": "white | black | null",
    "target_piece": "pawn | knight | ... | null",
    "target_square": "e5 | null",
    "is_sacrifice": false,
    "is_en_passant": false
  },
  "target_ply_hint": null
}
```

All fields are optional. A minimal claim is just `is_sacrifice: true` (which becomes a generic "a sacrifice occurred" claim). A maximal claim specifies everything.

---

## 2.2 What the LLM Extracts

### Example extractions

| English | Extracted params |
|---|---|
| "The bishop captures the rook." | `{color: null, piece: "bishop", target_piece: "rook"}` |
| "White's knight captures the black queen on d8." | `{color: "white", piece: "knight", target_color: "black", target_piece: "queen", target_square: "d8"}` |
| "Black sacrifices the exchange." | `{color: "black", is_sacrifice: true}` (target piece unspecified) |
| "The pawn captures en passant." | `{color: null, piece: "pawn", is_en_passant: true}` |
| "The king takes the bishop." | `{color: null, piece: "king", target_piece: "bishop"}` |

### Inferring sacrifice

`is_sacrifice: true` is set when the sentence explicitly says "sacrifices", "gives up", "offers", or similar. The LLM does **not** judge whether the sacrifice is sound; it only reports whether the sentence characterizes the capture as a sacrifice. The verifier does not use this field for verification — it is purely metadata for the report.

---

## 2.3 Verification Rule

### 2.3.1 Algorithm

```text
1. Collect all plies where:
   - ply.is_capture == True
   - claim.color is null OR ply.mover == claim.color
   - claim.piece is null OR ply.piece_type == piece_code(claim.piece)
   - claim.target_color is null OR ply.captured_color == claim.target_color
   - claim.target_piece is null OR ply.captured_piece == piece_code(claim.target_piece)
   - claim.target_square is null OR ply.to_square == square_index(claim.target_square)
   - claim.is_en_passant is false OR ply.is_en_passant == claim.is_en_passant
2. Apply the same single/zero/multiple logic as move descriptions.
```

### 2.3.2 Special handling: sacrifice claims

A claim with `is_sacrifice: true` is verified like any other capture claim — the system checks whether the capture occurred. The "sacrifice" label is not verified (the system does not judge whether the capture was a sound sacrifice). The verdict explanation does mention the sacrifice label so the author knows what was claimed.

## 2.4 Worked Examples

### Example 1: Clean verified

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "bishop", target_piece: "pawn", target_square: "h7"}`
**Facts**: Ply 12 is `Bxh7+` (white bishop captures black pawn on h7, giving check).
**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 12 is Bxh7+, a white bishop capturing a black pawn on h7 with check."

### Example 2: Subject ambiguity

**Claim**: `{color: null, piece: null, target_piece: "bishop", target_color: "white"}` (i.e., "Black captures the bishop" with color inferred from context)
**Facts**: Ply 13 is `Kxh7` (black king captures white bishop on h7).
**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 13 is Kxh7, the black king capturing the white bishop on h7."

If the actual move was `Kh8` (declined), the verdict becomes:

**Verdict**: Contradicted. "No capture of the white bishop was found at ply 13. The actual move was Kh8, declining the sacrifice. The bishop is still on h7 after ply 13."

### Example 3: Wrong captured piece

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "bishop", target_piece: "rook"}`
**Facts**: Ply 12 is `Bxh7+` capturing a pawn, not a rook.
**Verdict**: Contradicted. "No white bishop capturing a rook was found. A white bishop captured a pawn at ply 12 (Bxh7+). Did you mean pawn?"

### Example 4: Sacrifice declined

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "bishop", is_sacrifice: true}` ("The bishop is sacrificed.")
**Facts**: Ply 12 is `Bxh7+`.
**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 12 is Bxh7+, a white bishop capturing on h7. The sentence characterizes this as a sacrifice; the verifier confirms the capture occurred but does not evaluate sacrifice soundness."

### Example 5: Sacrifice declined by opponent

**Claim**: `{color: "black", target_piece: "bishop", target_color: "white"}` ("Black captures the bishop.")
**Facts**: Ply 13 is `Kh8` (Black declines).
**Verdict**: Contradicted. "Black did not capture the white bishop. The move at ply 13 was Kh8, declining the sacrifice. The white bishop remains on h7."

### Example 6: Hallucinated capture

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", target_piece: "queen", target_square: "d8"}`
**Facts**: No knight ever reached d8 in the game.
**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "No white knight capturing a queen on d8 was found at any ply. Closest match: a white knight moved to e5 at ply 14 (Ne5), but did not capture a queen."

## 2.5 Special Cases

### 2.5.1 En passant

"The pawn captures en passant" sets `is_en_passant: true`. python-chess exposes this via `board.is_en_passant(move)`. The facts database records it as `ply.is_en_passant`. The verifier checks the flag.

Note that the captured pawn is **not on the destination square** in en passant. The destination is the empty square behind the captured pawn. This can confuse authors ("the pawn captures on d6" when the captured pawn was on d5). The verifier's explanation should clarify: "The move at ply 18 is exd6 e.p., a white pawn capturing the black pawn on d5 via en passant, landing on d6."

### 2.5.2 King captures

Kings can capture. This is rare but legal. The verifier handles it like any other capture; no special code path.

### 2.5.3 Capturing the queen

Capturing the queen is a high-value event and often mentioned in commentary. The verifier handles it like any other capture, but the explanation should emphasize the material gain ("capturing the black queen on d8").

### 2.5.4 Recapture

"White recaptures" implies a capture on the same square where the previous move was a capture by the opponent. The verifier can detect this by checking whether `ply[N].to_square == ply[N-1].to_square` and both plies are captures. If the LLM extracts a recapture claim, the verifier should confirm the recapture pattern.

---

## 2.6 Common LLM Mistakes

### 2.6.1 Inventing the capturing piece

"The bishop captures the rook" when the actual capturer was the queen. The LLM guesses the piece type from context and is often wrong. The verifier's `Contradicted` verdict should propose the actual capturer: "A queen captured the rook at ply 18 (Qxe8), not a bishop."

### 2.6.2 Inventing the captured piece

"The bishop captures the rook" when the bishop actually captured a pawn. Similar to above. The verifier proposes the actual captured piece.

### 2.6.3 Inventing the capture entirely

"The bishop captures the rook" when no capture occurred at all in that part of the game. This is a `Possible hallucination` verdict, and it is one of the most important to flag — it indicates the LLM has lost track of the game.

### 2.6.4 Confusing sacrifice with material gain

"White sacrifices the bishop" sets `is_sacrifice: true`, but the LLM sometimes also sets `target_piece: "bishop"` and `color: "white"` (i.e., White captures its own bishop). The validator should reject this as a schema violation: a piece cannot capture itself.

### 2.6.5 Mismatched colors

"White's bishop captures the black queen" with `color: "white"` and `target_color: "black"` is well-formed. But the LLM sometimes produces `color: "white"` and `target_color: "white"` ("White captures White's queen"), which is impossible. The validator should reject.

---

## 2.7 Sacrifice Verification (Out of Scope for v1)

A sacrifice is, by definition, a deliberate material loss for compensation. Verifying that a capture was **actually a sacrifice** (rather than just a capture that lost material) requires:

- Confirming the capturing side lost material in the exchange.
- Confirming the capturing side gained compensation (positional or tactical).

This is a v2+ feature. For v1, sacrifice claims are verified only on the capture portion; the "sacrifice" label is preserved in the verdict explanation but not itself verified.

---

## 2.8 Tips for Authors

- **Specify both colors** when possible: "White's bishop captures Black's rook" is unambiguous.
- **Specify the square** for high-value captures: "captures the rook on a8".
- **Mention en passant explicitly** to avoid confusion.
- **Use "recaptures"** when applicable; the verifier recognizes the pattern.
- **Distinguish "sacrifices" from "captures"**: a sacrifice implies deliberate material loss; if the capture won material, it is not a sacrifice.

---

## 2.9 Key Reminders

- Capture claims involve two pieces (capturer and captured) plus optional color, square, sacrifice, and en-passant fields.
- Verification rule: find a ply where `is_capture == True` and every non-null field matches.
- Sacrifice is metadata, not verified. The system confirms the capture; it does not judge sacrifice soundness.
- En passant is special: the captured pawn is not on the destination square. The verifier's explanation should clarify this.
- Common LLM mistakes: inventing the capturer, inventing the captured piece, inventing the capture entirely, confusing sacrifice with material gain, mismatched colors.
- Validator should reject impossible combinations (same color capturing itself).
- v1 verifies capture occurrence only; v2+ will verify sacrifice soundness.
- Author tips: specify both colors, mention the square for high-value captures, use "recaptures" when applicable.
