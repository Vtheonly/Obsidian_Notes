# 2. Exercise Move Description Verification

> **The exercise in one sentence**: A detailed, step-by-step walkthrough of verifying five move description claims of increasing complexity — from a simple pawn push to a complex castling-and-promotion combination — with every verification step shown explicitly and every edge case explained.

This exercise is the most thorough in the vault. Read it slowly. Work through each step yourself before reading the answer.

## 2.2 Claim 1: Simple Pawn Move

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "pawn", destination_square: "e4"}` (i.e., "White plays e4" or "The pawn moves to e4")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find a ply that matches all the non-null fields:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == pawn`
- `to_square == e4`

### Worked solution

Scan the facts database for matching plies:

- Ply 1: mover=white, piece_type=pawn, to=e4. **Match.**
- Ply 5: mover=white, piece_type=pawn, to=d4. No (to_square != e4).
- Ply 19: mover=white, piece_type=pawn, to=a4. No.

Only one match: ply 1.

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 1 is e4, a white pawn moving to e4."

### Tips

- Always scan ALL plies. Do not stop at the first match — there could be multiple.
- The destination square is the **to_square**, not the from_square.
- "White's pawn to e4" means a white pawn moved to e4. The pawn could have come from anywhere (typically e2, but could be a capture like dxe4).

### Common mistakes

- **Confusing from and to.** "Pawn to e4" means `to_square == e4`, not `from_square == e4`.
- **Missing multiple matches.** If the pawn returned to e4 later in the game, there would be multiple matches. The verifier would mark `Ambiguous` without a hint.

## 2.3 Claim 2: Knight Move with Capture

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination_square: "d4", is_capture: true}` (i.e., "The white knight captures on d4")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == knight`
- `to_square == d4`
- `is_capture == true`

### Worked solution

Scan:

- Ply 3: mover=white, piece=knight, to=f3, is_capture=false. No.
- Ply 7: mover=white, piece=knight, to=d4, is_capture=true. **Match.**
- Ply 9: mover=white, piece=knight, to=c3, is_capture=false. No.
- Ply 13: mover=white, piece=knight, to=b3, is_capture=false. No.

Only one match: ply 7.

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 7 is Nxd4, a white knight capturing a black pawn on d4."

### Tips

- For capture claims, check both `piece_type` and `is_capture`. A non-capturing knight move to d4 would not match.
- The captured piece is `pawn` in this case. The claim does not specify the target piece, so any captured piece would match. If the claim specified `target_piece: "queen"`, the verifier would check that `captured_piece == queen` and emit `Contradicted` if it was a pawn.

### Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to check `is_capture`.** A knight moving to d4 without capturing would not match this claim.
- **Assuming the captured piece.** The claim does not say what was captured. The verifier should not assume; it should accept any captured piece.

## 2.4 Claim 3: Castling

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "king", is_castling: true}` (i.e., "White castles kingside")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == king`
- `is_castling == true`

### Worked solution

Scan:

- Ply 15: mover=white, piece=king, is_castling=true. **Match.**

Only one match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 15 is O-O, white castling kingside. The king moves from e1 to g1 and the rook from h1 to f1."

### Tips

- Castling is recorded as a king move in python-chess. `board.is_castling(move)` returns True.
- The destination square (g1 for kingside, c1 for queenside) is implicit in the castling flag. The claim does not need to specify it.
- "Castles kingside" vs. "castles queenside": the verifier should distinguish. If the claim said `is_castling: true` without specifying side, both would match. If the claim specifies side (e.g., via a `castling_side` field), the verifier checks `to_square == g1` for kingside or `to_square == c1` for queenside.

### Common mistakes

- **Confusing king moves with castling.** A king moving from e1 to f1 is a normal king move, not castling. The verifier checks `is_castling`, not just `piece_type == king`.
- **Forgetting that castling involves two pieces.** The rook also moves. The verifier typically only records the king's move, but the explanation should mention the rook.

## 2.5 Claim 4: Multiple Matches (Ambiguous)

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination_square: "f3"}` (i.e., "The knight moves to f3")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == knight`
- `to_square == f3`

### Worked solution

Scan:

- Ply 3: mover=white, piece=knight, to=f3. **Match.**

Hmm, only one match in this game. Let me adjust the example to make it ambiguous.

Suppose the game continued with the knight returning to f3 later. Add a hypothetical ply 21: `Nf3` (knight from b3 back to f3 — wait, that's not a legal knight move from b3. b3 to f3 is not a knight move. Let me pick a different scenario).

OK, let me adjust the game: suppose at ply 13, instead of Nb3, White plays Nde2 (knight from d4 to e2). Then at ply 21 (hypothetical), White plays Ng3 (knight from e2 to g3 — also not f3). Let me try: suppose at ply 11, instead of Be2, White plays Ng1 (knight from f3 back to g1, weird but legal). Then at ply 17, White plays Nf3 again (knight from g1 to f3). Now there are two `Nf3` moves: ply 3 and ply 17.

For this adjusted game, the scan finds:
- Ply 3: mover=white, piece=knight, to=f3. **Match.**
- Ply 17: mover=white, piece=knight, to=f3. **Match.**

Two matches.

**Verdict**: Ambiguous. "Multiple white knight moves to f3 found at plies 3 and 17. Provide a target_ply_hint or specify additional constraints (e.g., from_square) to disambiguate."

### Tips

- Knights can return to the same square multiple times in a long game. This is common.
- Always scan the full game. Do not assume there is only one match.
- The verdict should list all matching plies so the author can disambiguate.

### Common mistakes

- **Stopping at the first match.** Always scan all plies.
- **Choosing one match arbitrarily.** The verifier should not guess. Mark `Ambiguous` and let the author or LLM provide a hint.

## 2.6 Claim 5: Wrong Color

**Claim**: `{color: "black", piece: "knight", destination_square: "f3"}` (i.e., "Black's knight moves to f3")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == black`
- `piece_type == knight`
- `to_square == f3`

### Worked solution

Scan:

- Ply 4: mover=black, piece=pawn. No (wrong piece type).
- Ply 6: mover=black, piece=pawn. No.
- Ply 8: mover=black, piece=knight, to=f6. No (wrong square).
- Ply 10: mover=black, piece=pawn. No.
- Ply 12: mover=black, piece=pawn. No.
- Ply 14: mover=black, piece=bishop. No.
- Ply 16: mover=black, is_castling=true (king). No.
- Ply 18: mover=black, piece=queen. No.
- Ply 20: mover=black, piece=pawn. No.

No match.

**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "No black knight move to f3 was found in the game. Closest matches: black knight moved to f6 at ply 8 (Nf6). Did you mean Nf6?"

### Tips

- When no match is found, the verifier should propose close misses. "Did you mean Nf6?" is more useful than just "not found".
- Close misses are computed by relaxing one constraint at a time (e.g., wrong color, wrong piece type, wrong square) and finding the closest match.

### Common mistakes

- **Stopping at "not found".** Always provide close misses. They are often the actual error (typo in the claim).
- **Inventing a match.** The verifier should never fabricate a ply. If there is no match, say so.

## 2.7 Claim 6: Promotion

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "pawn", is_promotion: true, promotion_piece: "queen"}` (i.e., "The pawn promotes to a queen")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == pawn`
- `is_promotion == true`
- `promotion_piece == queen`

### Worked solution

Scan the facts database:

- No plies in this game involve promotion.

No match.

**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "No pawn promotion was found in the game. The game ends at ply 20 with no pawn having reached the 8th rank."

### Tips

- Promotion is rare. Most games do not have one.
- The verifier should check both `is_promotion` and `promotion_piece`. A promotion to a rook would not match `promotion_piece: queen`.
- The promotion piece is encoded in python-chess as `move.promotion` (e.g., `chess.QUEEN`).

### Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the promotion piece.** A claim "promotes to a queen" requires `promotion_piece == queen`. The verifier should check this, not just `is_promotion`.
- **Confusing pawn moves with promotions.** A pawn moving to the 8th rank without promoting is illegal. The verifier can assume `is_promotion == true` for any pawn move to the 8th rank, but it should still check the explicit flag for consistency with the claim.

## 2.8 Claim 7: Check-Related Claim

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "bishop", destination_square: "h7", is_check: true}` (i.e., "The white bishop gives check on h7")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == bishop`
- `to_square == h7`
- `is_check == true`

### Worked solution

Scan the facts database:

- Ply 11: mover=white, piece=bishop, to=e2, is_check=false. No.
- Ply 17: mover=white, piece=bishop, to=e3, is_check=false. No.

No bishop move to h7 in this game.

**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "No white bishop move to h7 with check was found in the game."

### Tips

- Check is a separate flag (`is_check`) from the move itself. A bishop can move to h7 without giving check (if the enemy king is not on the diagonal).
- The verifier should check `is_check` independently of the move pattern.

### Common mistakes

- **Assuming any bishop move to h7 gives check.** It depends on the king's position.
- **Confusing check with capture.** A move can be a capture without check, or check without capture, or both, or neither.

- `to_square == e3`

One match.

### What if the claim was wrong?

Suppose the claim was `{color: "white", piece: "knight", destination_square: "e3"}` (i.e., "The white knight moves to e3"). Then:

- No knight move to e3 in the game (the knight went to b3 at ply 13, not e3).
- Closest match: a bishop move to e3 at ply 17 (Be3). Did you mean bishop?

**Verdict**: Contradicted. "No white knight move to e3 was found. A white bishop moved to e3 at ply 17 (Be3). Did you mean bishop?"

### Tips

- When the piece type is wrong, propose the actual piece type in the explanation.
- The "close miss" pattern (wrong piece type, right square) is common and is one of the most useful patterns for authors.

### Common mistakes

- **Saying only "not found".** Always propose the actual piece type if a different piece moved to the claimed square.
- **Inventing a match.** The verifier should never fabricate a ply. If there is no match, say so.

## 2.11 Patterns to Remember

1. **Single match → Verified.** The most common case.
2. **No match → Possible hallucination** (with close misses in the explanation).
3. **Multiple matches → Ambiguous** (with all matching plies listed).
4. **Wrong piece type → Contradicted** (with the actual piece proposed).
5. **Wrong square → Contradicted** (with the actual square proposed if close).
6. **Special flags (check, capture, castling, promotion) → check each independently.**

---

## 2.12 Tips and Tricks

- Always scan ALL plies. Do not stop at the first match.
- Use close-miss detection to suggest corrections.
- Check special flags (`is_check`, `is_capture`, `is_castling`, `is_promotion`) independently.
- Distinguish "no match" (Possible hallucination) from "wrong match" (Contradicted).
- For multi-match cases, list all matching plies in the explanation.
- For castling, the destination square is implicit; do not require it in the claim.
- For promotion, check both `is_promotion` and `promotion_piece`.
- For check, check `is_check` independently of the move pattern.

---

## 2.13 Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Stopping at the first match.** Always scan all plies.
- **Inventing matches.** The verifier never fabricates plies.
- **Confusing from and to squares.** "Pawn to e4" means `to_square == e4`.
- **Forgetting special flags.** A capture claim must check `is_capture`; a check claim must check `is_check`.
- **Saying only "not found" without close misses.** Always propose corrections.
- **Choosing arbitrarily in ambiguous cases.** Mark `Ambiguous` and list all matches.
- **Assuming the captured piece for capture claims.** The claim must specify; otherwise, any captured piece matches.

## 2.14 Key Reminders

- Move description verification is the simplest claim type and the most common.
- Eight worked examples covering: simple move, capture, castling, ambiguous, hallucination, promotion, check, wrong piece type.
- Six patterns: single match → Verified; no match → Possible hallucination; multiple matches → Ambiguous; wrong piece → Contradicted; wrong square → Contradicted; special flags checked independently.
- Always scan all plies. Always provide close misses. Always check special flags.
- The verifier never fabricates plies. When in doubt, mark Ambiguous or Cannot verify.
- This exercise is the foundation for all other claim types. Master it before moving on.
