# 5. Exercise Material Claim Verification

> **The exercise in one sentence**: A detailed, step-by-step walkthrough of verifying five material claims — single-ply gain, move-sequence gain, game-total gain, material-equal, and hallucinated gain — using the material timeline in the facts database.

Material claims are the third most common type and the easiest to verify after move descriptions. This exercise solidifies the verification rules from Chapter 4.5.

## 5.1 Setup

You are the verifier. Here is the material timeline for a test game:

## 5.2 Claim 1: Single-Ply Pawn Win

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `{gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "knight", count: 1, window: "single_ply"}`, target_ply_hint: 7

### Your task

Verify:
- Compute delta between ply 6 and ply 7.
- Check whether delta matches `count * piece_value(piece_won)`.

### Worked solution

- material_timeline[7].delta = +3
- material_timeline[6].delta = 0
- Change at ply 7 = +3 - 0 = +3
- Expected: count * piece_value(knight) = 1 * 3 = 3
- Match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "At ply 7, the material balance shifts from 0 to +3 for White. White captured a black knight (Bxc6). The claim that White wins a knight is verified."

### Tips

- For single-ply claims, the change is `delta[ply] - delta[ply-1]`.
- The gaining color is from the perspective of the delta: positive delta change means White gained; negative means Black gained.
- If `gaining_color` is Black, flip the sign: `delta_change_for_black = -(delta[ply] - delta[ply-1])`.

### Common mistakes

- **Forgetting to flip the sign for Black.** Delta is from White's perspective. If gaining_color is Black, multiply by -1.
- **Confusing "winning material" with "winning the piece".** "White wins a knight" means White's material balance increased by 3 (a knight's value), which usually means Black lost a knight and White did not lose anything of equal value.

## 5.3 Claim 2: Move-Sequence Rook Win

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `{gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "rook", count: 1, window: "move_sequence"}`, target_ply_hint: 7, window_end_ply_hint: 16

### Your task

Verify:
- Compute delta between ply 7 and ply 16.
- Check whether delta matches `count * piece_value(rook)`.

### Worked solution

- material_timeline[16].delta = -1
- material_timeline[7].delta = +3
- Change from ply 7 to ply 16 = -1 - 3 = -4 (from White's perspective, White lost 4 pawns worth of material relative to ply 7)
- For White as the gaining color: delta_change_for_white = -4
- Expected: 1 * 5 = +5
- No match.

Wait, let me re-examine. At ply 7, delta = +3 (White up a knight). At ply 16, delta = -1 (Black up a pawn). So White went from +3 to -1, a change of -4 (White lost material relative to ply 7).

The claim says White wins a rook (worth +5). The actual change is -4. So the claim is contradicted.

**Verdict**: Contradicted. "Between plies 7 and 16, the material balance shifted from +3 to -1 for White (a change of -4). White did not win a rook; in fact, White lost material over this sequence. The claim is contradicted."

### Tips

- For move-sequence claims, the change is `delta[end] - delta[start]`.
- If the change is negative (for the gaining color), the color actually lost material.
- Always report the actual change and the expected change, so the author can see the discrepancy.

### Common mistakes

- **Reporting only "not verified" without numbers.** Always include the actual delta change and the expected value.
- **Forgetting to consider intermediate material changes.** A sequence might involve both gains and losses; the net change is what matters for the claim.

## 5.4 Claim 3: Game-Total Material Equal

**Claim**: `material_equal` with target_ply_hint: 16 (the last ply of the game)

### Your task

Verify:
- Check whether `delta` at the target ply is 0.

### Worked solution

- material_timeline[16].delta = -1
- Not 0.

**Verdict**: Contradicted. "At ply 16, material is -1 (Black is up one pawn). The claim that material is equal is contradicted."

### Tips

- For material-equal claims, just check whether delta is 0 at the target ply.
- If the claim does not specify a target ply, use the last ply of the game.
- Be precise about the discrepancy: "Black is up one pawn" is more useful than "material is not equal".

### Common mistakes

- **Saying only "not equal".** Always specify which side is up and by how much.
- **Using the wrong ply.** Make sure the target ply is the one the claim refers to. If the claim is about the end of the game, use the last ply.

## 5.5 Claim 4: Hallucinated Material Gain

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `{gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "queen", count: 1, window: "move_sequence"}`, target_ply_hint: 7, window_end_ply_hint: 16

### Your task

Verify:
- Compute delta between ply 7 and ply 16.
- Check whether delta matches `count * piece_value(queen)`.

### Worked solution

- Change from ply 7 to ply 16 = -4 (from White's perspective)
- For White as the gaining color: delta_change_for_white = -4
- Expected: 1 * 9 = +9
- No match. In fact, the change is negative (White lost material).

**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "Between plies 7 and 16, material balance shifted from +3 to -1 for White (a change of -4). White did not win a queen; in fact, White lost material over this sequence. The claim appears fabricated."

### Tips

- When the actual change is opposite in sign to the expected change, mark as `Possible hallucination` rather than `Contradicted`.
- `Contradicted` means "a similar but smaller gain occurred"; `Possible hallucination` means "nothing like this happened at all".
- The distinction matters: `Possible hallucination` is a stronger warning that suggests the LLM has lost track of the game.

### Common mistakes

- **Using `Contradicted` for hallucinations.** Use `Possible hallucination` when the actual change is opposite in sign to the expected change.
- **Forgetting to flip the sign for Black.** If gaining_color is Black and the actual change is +4 (for White), then for Black the change is -4.

## 5.6 Claim 5: Material Equal at the Start

**Claim**: `material_equal` with target_ply_hint: 0

### Your task

Verify:
- Check whether `delta` at ply 0 is 0.

### Worked solution

- material_timeline[0].delta = 0
- Match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "At ply 0 (the starting position), material is equal (39 vs 39)."

### Tips

- This is a trivial case but worth testing. The verifier should handle ply 0 correctly.
- Ply 0 represents the starting position before any moves. Delta is always 0 here (assuming standard chess).

### Common mistakes

- **Off-by-one errors.** Ply 0 is the starting position. Ply 1 is after White's first move. Make sure the indexing is consistent with the facts database.

## 5.7 Claim 6: Winning the Exchange

**Claim**: `material_gain` with `{gaining_color: "white", piece_won: "exchange", count: 1, window: "move_sequence"}`, target_ply_hint: 7, window_end_ply_hint: 16

### Your task

Verify:
- "Winning the exchange" means gaining a rook for a minor piece (knight or bishop), netting +2.
- Check whether the delta change matches +2 AND the gaining color captured a rook AND lost a minor piece.

### Worked solution

- Change from ply 7 to ply 16 = -4 (from White's perspective)
- For White: -4. Not +2.
- Also, White did not capture any rook in this game (no rook captures occurred).
- The exchange pattern does not apply.

**Verdict**: Contradicted. "Between plies 7 and 16, White did not win the exchange. In fact, no rook captures occurred in this game, and White's material balance decreased by 4. The claim is contradicted."

### Tips

- "Winning the exchange" is a specific pattern: rook for minor piece, net +2.
- The verifier should check both: (a) the delta change is +2, AND (b) the gaining color captured a rook, AND (c) the gaining color lost a minor piece.
- If only the delta matches but the pattern does not, the verifier should report the discrepancy.

### Common mistakes

- **Treating "winning the exchange" as just "+2 delta".** The pattern requires a rook capture and a minor piece loss. The verifier should check both.
- **Forgetting to check the actual pieces captured.** A +2 delta could result from two pawn captures and a knight capture, not necessarily the exchange pattern.

## 5.9 Patterns to Remember

1. **Single-ply gain**: delta[ply] - delta[ply-1] should equal `count * piece_value(piece_won)`.
2. **Move-sequence gain**: delta[end] - delta[start] should equal `count * piece_value(piece_won)`.
3. **Game-total gain**: delta[last] - delta[0] should equal `count * piece_value(piece_won)`.
4. **Material equal**: delta[ply] should be 0.
5. **Hallucination**: actual change is opposite in sign to expected change → `Possible hallucination`.
6. **Contradiction**: actual change is same sign but wrong magnitude, OR the pattern (e.g., exchange) does not apply → `Contradicted`.

## 5.10 Tips and Tricks

- Use the material timeline in the facts database for O(1) lookup. Do not recount pieces.
- For Black as the gaining color, flip the sign of the delta change.
- For "winning the exchange", check both the delta (+2) and the pattern (rook captured, minor piece lost).
- When the actual change is opposite in sign to the expected change, mark as `Possible hallucination`.
- When the actual change is the same sign but wrong magnitude, mark as `Contradicted`.
- Always include the actual delta change and the expected value in the explanation.
