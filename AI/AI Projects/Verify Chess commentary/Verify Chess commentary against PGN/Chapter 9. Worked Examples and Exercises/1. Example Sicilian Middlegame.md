# 1. Example Sicilian Middlegame

> **The example in one sentence**: A complete walkthrough of the verifier on a single chess note — from Markdown intake to per-claim verdict report — using the canonical Sicilian Middlegame example from the project overview.

This note is the canonical end-to-end example. Every step of the pipeline is shown explicitly, with the intermediate artifacts. Use it as a reference for what the verifier should produce on a real note.

## 1.1 The Input Note

```markdown

This example shows a typical Greek Gift sacrifice against the Sicilian.

```pgn

7. Bxh7+

White sacrifices the bishop on h7 to expose the king.

Black accepts the sacrifice.

The king becomes exposed.

The queen joins the attack.

White wins a rook.

The note contains:
- A header (line 1).
- An intro paragraph (line 3).
- A PGN block (lines 5-13).
- Five prose sentences (lines 15, 17, 19, 21, 23), each making one or more factual claims.

## 1.6 Step 5: Verification (Track A + Track B → Verdicts)

The verifier processes each claim:

### Claim 1: "White sacrifices the bishop on h7 to expose the king."

**Type**: capture
**Search**: plies where `mover == white`, `piece_type == bishop`, `is_capture == True`, `to_square == h7`.

**Match**: Ply 13 (`Bxh7+`).

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 13 is Bxh7+, a white bishop capturing a black pawn on h7 with check. The sentence characterizes this as a sacrifice; the verifier confirms the capture occurred but does not evaluate sacrifice soundness."

### Claim 2: "Black accepts the sacrifice."

**Type**: capture
**Search**: plies where `mover == black`, `is_capture == True`, `captured_piece == bishop`, `captured_color == white`.

**Match**: ??? The PGN ends at ply 13. There is no ply 14.

This is the critical moment. The verifier must handle the case where the claim refers to a ply that does not exist in the PGN.

**Verdict**: Cannot verify automatically. "The PGN ends at ply 13 (after Bxh7+). The claim 'Black accepts the sacrifice' refers to ply 14, which is not present in the provided PGN. To verify this claim, the PGN must be extended to include Black's response."

**Alternative interpretation**: if the verifier is lenient, it might emit `Possible hallucination` instead, since no evidence exists for the claim. But the more accurate verdict is `Cannot verify automatically`, because the issue is missing data, not a factual contradiction.

### Claim 3: "The king becomes exposed."

**Type**: strategic_king_safety
**Verification**: compute king safety score at ply 12 (before the sacrifice) and ply 13 (after).

- Ply 12: Black king on g8, pawn shield f7-g7-h7 = 3, no attackers in king zone = 0, score = 3.
- Ply 13: Black king on g8 (still), pawn shield f7-g7 = 2 (h7 pawn was captured), enemy attackers in king zone (bishop on h7 attacks g8) = 1, score = 1.

Score drop: 3 → 1, delta = -2. Threshold for "worsened significantly" is -5; "worsened" is -2 or more. So the claim is supported at the "worsened" level.

Engine eval confirms: 0.4 → 1.8, delta = +1.4 (from White's perspective, indicating Black's position worsened).

**Verdict**: Supported (soft). "Black's king safety score dropped from +3 (pawn shield f7-g7-h7, no attackers) to +1 (pawn shield reduced to f7-g7, white bishop attacking g8). Engine eval jumped from +0.4 to +1.8, confirming the worsened king safety. The claim that the king became exposed is supported."

### Claim 4: "The queen joins the attack."

**Type**: move_description
**Search**: plies where `mover == white`, `piece_type == queen`.

**Match**: ??? The PGN ends at ply 13. White's next move (ply 15, since ply 14 would be Black's response) is not in the PGN.

**Verdict**: Cannot verify automatically. "The PGN ends at ply 13. The claim 'The queen joins the attack' refers to a subsequent white queen move, which is not in the provided PGN."

### Claim 5: "White wins a rook."

**Type**: material_gain
**Verification**: compare material at ply 13 (the sacrifice) to material at the end of the game.

But the PGN ends at ply 13. There is no "end of game" material count.

**Verdict**: Cannot verify automatically. "The PGN ends at ply 13. The claim 'White wins a rook' requires material comparison across multiple plies that are not in the provided PGN."

## 1.8 What This Example Reveals

This canonical example reveals several important properties of the verifier:

1. **It handles incomplete PGNs gracefully.** Three of the five claims cannot be verified because the PGN is incomplete. The verifier does not crash; it emits `Cannot verify automatically` with a clear explanation.

2. **It distinguishes binary facts from soft claims.** Claim 1 (capture) is binary and gets `Verified`. Claim 3 (king safety) is soft and gets `Supported (soft)` with a confidence score.

3. **It cites specific evidence.** Every verdict references a specific ply, SAN, FEN, or eval. The author can verify the verdict by checking the cited evidence.

4. **It does not invent facts.** The verifier does not guess what happened after ply 13. It says `Cannot verify automatically` rather than making up a verdict.

5. **It produces actionable recommendations.** The final "Notes" section tells the author exactly what to do (extend the PGN) to enable verification of the remaining claims.
