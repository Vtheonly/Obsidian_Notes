**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 7 is Bxc6, a white bishop capturing the black knight on c6."

### Tips

- For capture claims, all six fields must match: mover color, mover piece, is_capture, captured piece, captured color, target square.
- The target square is the **to_square** of the move, which is where the capturing piece lands (and where the captured piece was).
- For en passant, the target square is NOT where the captured piece was (see Claim 5).

### Common mistakes

- **Confusing target_square with captured_square.** For normal captures, they are the same. For en passant, they are different.
- **Forgetting to check `is_capture`.** A bishop moving to c6 without capturing would not match.
- **Forgetting to check the captured piece type.** If the bishop had captured a rook instead of a knight, the claim would be `Contradicted`.

- `captured_color == white`
- `to_square == c6`

### Worked solution

Scan:

- Ply 8: mover=black, piece=pawn, is_capture=true, captured=bishop, captured_color=white, to=c6. **Match.**

One match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 8 is dxc6, a black pawn recapturing the white bishop on c6. This is a recapture because the previous ply (ply 7, Bxc6) was also a capture on c6."

### Tips

- The verifier can detect recaptures by checking whether `ply[N].to_square == ply[N-1].to_square` and both plies are captures. Mention this in the explanation.
- "Recapture" is not a separate claim type; it is a capture claim with the recapture pattern detected by the verifier.

### Common mistakes

- **Treating recapture as a separate type.** It is just a capture that happens to follow another capture on the same square.
- **Missing the recapture pattern.** The verifier should mention "recapture" in the explanation when the pattern is detected, but the verdict label is still `Verified` (for a capture claim).

## 3.5 Claim 4: Hallucinated Capture

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "rook", target_piece: "queen", target_color: "black", target_square: "d8"}` (i.e., "The white rook captures the black queen on d8")

**Target ply hint**: none

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == rook`
- `is_capture == true`
- `captured_piece == queen`
- `captured_color == black`
- `to_square == d8`

### Worked solution

Scan all plies:

- No rook moves by White in this game (White castled at ply 9, but castling is not a rook move in the move list — actually it is, but it's a king move with the rook moving implicitly).
- No captures of a black queen in this game.

No match.

**Verdict**: Possible hallucination. "No white rook capturing the black queen on d8 was found in the game. In fact, no rook moves by White and no captures of the black queen occurred. This claim appears to be fabricated."

### Tips

- When no match exists AND no similar capture exists, mark as `Possible hallucination` rather than `Contradicted`.
- `Contradicted` means "a similar but different capture occurred"; `Possible hallucination` means "nothing like this happened at all".
- The distinction matters: `Possible hallucination` is a stronger warning that suggests the LLM has lost track of the game.

### Common mistakes

- **Using `Contradicted` for hallucinations.** Use `Possible hallucination` when no similar event occurred.
- **Using `Possible hallucination` for wrong details.** Use `Contradicted` when a similar event occurred but with different details.

## 3.6 Claim 5: En Passant

**Claim**: `{color: "white", piece: "pawn", target_piece: "pawn", target_color: "black", target_square: "f6", is_en_passant: true}` (i.e., "White captures en passant on f6")

**Target ply hint**: none (using Game 2)

### Your task

Find plies matching:
- `mover == white`
- `piece_type == pawn`
- `is_capture == true`
- `is_en_passant == true`
- `captured_piece == pawn`
- `captured_color == black`
- `to_square == f6`

### Worked solution

Scan Game 2:

- Ply 5: mover=white, piece=pawn, is_capture=true, is_en_passant=true, captured=pawn, captured_color=black, to=f6. **Match.**

One match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 5 is exf6 e.p., a white pawn capturing the black pawn on f5 via en passant, landing on f6. Note that the captured pawn was on f5, not f6 — en passant captures land on the empty square behind the captured pawn."

### Tips

- For en passant, the captured pawn is NOT on the destination square. The destination is the empty square behind the captured pawn (one rank further from the capturer's starting rank).
- Always explain en passant in the verdict: "the captured pawn was on f5, not f6". Authors often confuse this.
- The `is_en_passant` flag is essential. Without it, the verifier would not know to look for the captured pawn on a different square.

### Common mistakes

- **Assuming the captured piece is on the destination square.** For en passant, it is not.
- **Forgetting to mention en passant in the explanation.** The author needs to know this was an e.p. capture to understand the verdict.
- **Confusing e.p. with normal captures.** A normal pawn capture would not have `is_en_passant: true`.

- `captured_color == black`
- `to_square == h7`

(`is_sacrifice` is metadata, not used for matching.)

### Worked solution

Scan:

- Ply 13 of the Sicilian Middlegame game: Bxh7+. **Match.**

One match.

**Verdict**: Verified. "The move at ply 13 is Bxh7+, a white bishop capturing a black pawn on h7 with check. The sentence characterizes this as a sacrifice; the verifier confirms the capture occurred but does not evaluate sacrifice soundness."

### Tips

- `is_sacrifice` is metadata. It does not affect the matching.
- The explanation should mention that the sacrifice label is not verified. The system confirms the capture; it does not judge whether the sacrifice was sound.
- v1 does not verify sacrifice soundness. v2+ might, by checking whether the capturing side lost material in the resulting sequence.

### Common mistakes

- **Trying to verify sacrifice soundness.** v1 does not do this. The sacrifice label is preserved in the explanation but not itself verified.
- **Forgetting to mention the sacrifice label in the explanation.** The author needs to know the system recorded the label.

## 3.9 Patterns to Remember

1. **All fields match → Verified.** The most common case.
2. **Wrong captured piece → Contradicted** with the actual captured piece proposed.
3. **No similar capture → Possible hallucination.** Stronger warning than `Contradicted`.
4. **Recapture → Verified** with "recapture" mentioned in the explanation.
5. **En passant → Verified** with the captured pawn's actual square explained.
6. **Sacrifice → Verified** (capture portion) with the sacrifice label preserved but not verified.

---

## 3.10 Tips and Tricks

- For capture claims, six fields must match: mover color, mover piece, is_capture, captured piece, captured color, target square.
- For en passant, also check `is_en_passant`. The captured pawn is on a different square.
- For sacrifices, `is_sacrifice` is metadata. Do not use it for matching.
- For recaptures, detect the pattern (consecutive captures on the same square) and mention it in the explanation.
- When the captured piece is wrong, propose the actual captured piece.
- When no similar capture exists, mark as `Possible hallucination` (not `Contradicted`).
- Always include `is_en_passant` in the explanation when relevant. Authors often confuse the captured pawn's location.

---

## 3.11 Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Confusing target_square with captured_square** for en passant.
- **Forgetting to check `is_capture`.** A piece moving to a square is not necessarily a capture.
- **Reporting "not found" without close misses.** Always propose corrections.
- **Trying to verify sacrifice soundness in v1.** v1 only verifies the capture.
- **Using `Contradicted` for hallucinations.** Use `Possible hallucination` when no similar event occurred.
- **Missing the recapture pattern.** Mention "recapture" in the explanation when detected.

---

## 3.12 Key Reminders

- Capture claims involve two pieces (capturer and captured) plus optional color, square, sacrifice, and en-passant fields.
- Six worked examples: clean capture, recapture, wrong captured piece, hallucinated capture, en passant, sacrifice.
- Six patterns: all match → Verified; wrong captured piece → Contradicted; no similar → Possible hallucination; recapture → Verified with note; en passant → Verified with explanation; sacrifice → Verified (capture only).
- En passant is special: the captured pawn is not on the destination square.
- Sacrifice is metadata: the system confirms the capture; it does not judge sacrifice soundness in v1.
- This exercise covers the second most common claim type. Master it after move descriptions.
