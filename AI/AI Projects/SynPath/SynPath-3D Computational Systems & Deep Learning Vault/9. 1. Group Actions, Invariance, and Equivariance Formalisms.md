### 1. Concept Definition & Intuition
A **group $(G, \cdot)$** is a mathematical set paired with an associative binary operation satisfying identity, inverse, and closure. 

A **group action** describes how group elements transform states in a vector space $X$. For $g \in G$ and $x \in X$, the action is denoted $g \cdot x$ or $T_g(x)$.

In 3D geometric deep learning, we consider the Lie group $\mathrm{SE}(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes \mathrm{SO}(3)$ acting on coordinate point clouds $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}$:
$$T_g(\mathbf{X}) = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^T + \vec{t}, \quad \mathbf{R} \in \mathrm{SO}(3), \, \vec{t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$

Two fundamental symmetries govern geometric modeling:
1. **$\mathrm{SE}(3)$-Invariance**: A mapping $f: X \to Y$ is invariant if transforming the input leaves the output unchanged:
   $$f(T_g(\mathbf{X})) = f(\mathbf{X}) \quad \forall g \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$$
   *Biophysical Example*: Predicted binding affinity $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ must be strictly invariant to global rotation or translation of the protein-ligand complex.
2. **$\mathrm{SE}(3)$-Equivariance**: A mapping $f: X \to Y$ is equivariant if transforming the input transforms the output by an equivalent group action $S_g$:
   $$f(T_g(\mathbf{X})) = S_g(f(\mathbf{X})) \quad \forall g \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$$
   *Biophysical Example*: Predicted atomic forces $\vec{F}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ or synthon attachment exit vectors $\vec{v}_u \in \mathbb{S}^2$ must rotate along with the complex:
   $$\vec{v}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}^T + \vec{t}) = \mathbf{R} \vec{v}(\mathbf{X})$$
