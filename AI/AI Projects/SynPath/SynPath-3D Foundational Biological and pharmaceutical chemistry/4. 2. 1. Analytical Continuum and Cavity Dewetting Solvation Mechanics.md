# 4.2.1. Analytical Continuum and Cavity Dewetting Solvation Mechanics

## Physical decomposition

Aqueous ligand binding contains receptor-ligand interaction, desolvation, receptor/ligand reorganization and solvent-release contributions:

\[
\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}
=
\Delta G_{\mathrm{interaction}}
+
\Delta G_{\mathrm{desolvation}}
+
\Delta G_{\mathrm{reorganization}}
+
\Delta G_{\mathrm{solvent\,release}}.
\]

The implementation must choose and document one sign convention.

## Continuum component

Generalized Born / surface-area methods provide differentiable approximations to solvent polarization and non-polar surface effects.

A schematic GB contribution is:

\[
\Delta G_{\mathrm{GB}}
\propto
\sum_{i,j}
\frac{q_iq_j}
{f_{\mathrm{GB}}(r_{ij},R_i,R_j)}.
\]

This is an abstract form, not a complete force-field specification. Self terms, radii, atomic parameters and exclusions must match the selected GB implementation.

## Local hydration component

Water structure varies strongly near protein surfaces. Explicit-solvent/GIST-style analyses provide local density and energetic/entropic observables. HENF learns a continuous approximation:

\[
\Psi_\phi(r\mid\mathcal P)
\rightarrow
[\Delta\mu_{\mathrm{excess}}(r),g_O(r)].
\]

The production system queries HENF continuously instead of tying a frozen solvent grid to one conformation.

## Thresholds

A value such as \(+1.5\) kcal/mol may be used as an initial analysis threshold for candidate frustrated-water regions. It is a calibration parameter, not a universal physical constant.
