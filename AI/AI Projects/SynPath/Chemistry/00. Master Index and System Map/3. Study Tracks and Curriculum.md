# 3. Study Tracks and Curriculum

> [!abstract] Core Idea
> Four ways through the vault: the full 12-week curriculum, a chemistry-first track for readers who want the molecule story, an ML-first track for readers who want the model story, and an evaluation track for readers who only need to judge results. All tracks use the same dependency chains from [[2. Prerequisite Knowledge Graph]].

## 1. Track A — The Full Curriculum (12 Weeks)

The complete path for a reader starting from zero science background. Each week is roughly 4–6 study sessions; Essential notes first, Supporting notes in the same week they belong to, Advanced notes flagged for a second pass.

> [!tip] Weekly Ritual
> (1) Read the module MOC mind map first. (2) Read each note's sections 1–5 carefully, skim 6–9. (3) Answer the MOC's gate-check questions from memory. (4) Re-read the concept mind map of each note and reconstruct it on paper. (5) Revisit last week's gate check — spaced repetition is what makes it stick.

## 2. Track B — Chemistry-First (the molecule story)

For readers who want to understand **what the system builds** before how it learns. Read modules in this order: [[0. Chemistry Foundations MOC]] → [[0. Medicinal Chemistry and Synthesis MOC]] → [[0. Spatial Chemistry and Force Fields MOC]] → [[0. Structural Biology and Protein Targets MOC]] → [[0. Pharmacology and Drug Discovery MOC]], then finish with [[1. The Assembly MDP Formulation]] and [[3. Stationary Terminal Reward Formulation]] to see the chemistry formalized as decisions and rewards. The ML internals (PaiNN, GFlowNet theory) can wait for a second pass.

## 3. Track C — ML-First (the model story)

For readers comfortable with neural networks who want **how it learns** as fast as possible:

1. Minimum science context: [[3. Molecular Geometry and Hybridization]], [[1. Organic Functional Groups]], [[2. Rotatable Bonds and Dihedral Angles]], [[1. SMILES and Canonical Molecular Graphs]], [[1. Binding Pockets and Cavity Extraction]].
2. Then straight through [[0. Geometric Deep Learning and Equivariance MOC]], [[0. Circular Statistics and Torsion Heads MOC]], and [[0. Markov Decision Processes and GFlowNets MOC]].
3. Backfill the chemistry depth (reactions, synthons, force fields) as the Project Connection sections demand it.

> [!warning] Do Not Skip the Backfill
> The policy heads choose reactions and synthons; the torsion head rotates real bonds; the reward is a physical energy. Without the backfilled chemistry, every Project Connection section reads like trivia instead of design rationale. Track C is an ordering, not an exemption.

## 4. Track D — Evaluation and Translation

For readers who only need to **judge or replicate results**: [[2. Kinetic and Affinity Metrics IC50 Kd Ki]] → [[3. X-Ray Crystallography and PDB Artifacts]] → [[0. ## 6. How to Study a Single Note

1. **Read the abstract and the Concept Mind Map first** — they frame what you are about to learn.
2. **Section 1 (What It Is) and 2 (Why It Exists)** define the concept and the problem it solves. If either is unclear, the note's Prerequisites tell you what to revisit.
3. **Section 3 (How It Works)** is the core — work through the derivations and worked examples with a pencil.
4. **Section 5 (What You Must Understand)** collects the pitfalls and reminders; these are the exact points learners forget. Re-read this section the day after.
5. **Section 7 (Project Connection)** maps the concept onto code symbols, tensors, and design decisions of 3D-SynTree — whenever possible, open the cited file in the codebase and find it.
6. **Sections 6 and 9** place the note in the graph; follow one Further Dependency link forward and one Prerequisite link backward to keep the graph connected in your head.

## 7. Dual-Track Validation

For every computational concept, keep the wet-lab counterpart in mind: predicted poses are proven by X-ray co-crystals ([[2. SPR Kinetics and X-Ray Complex Confirmation]]), predicted torsions by observed dihedrals in those crystals, predicted affinity by SPR and ITC, synthesizability by the assay cascade's synthesis stage ([[1. Assay Cascade Strategy and nanoDSF]]). Understanding how in-silico tensors translate into physical experiments is the fastest way to spot when a computational shortcut is scientifically hollow.
