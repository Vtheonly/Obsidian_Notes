# 10.5. Project Conclusion — The Triple-Threat Architecture

The **Unified Medical Knowledge Architecture** for Rare Diseases is a hybrid pipeline that combines three distinct paradigms of AI — **Connectionist**, **Symbolic**, and **Supervised** — into a single, high-precision diagnostic system. This concluding note summarises the architecture, evaluates its success against the jury's requirements, and outlines the future work.

## 1. The Three-Layer Architecture

The project's pipeline is a **three-layer hybrid**, where each layer is assigned the task it is best at:

### 1.1. Layer 1 — Connectionist AI (Embeddings)

- **Components**: LLM Clinical Cleaner (Gemini/GPT), BioBERT, ClinicalBERT, PubMedBERT, SapBERT, MiniLM.
- **Role**: Pattern recognition at scale. The LLM reads free-text patient notes and extracts structured phenotypes; the embedding models produce 768-dim vectors for cosine-similarity retrieval.
- **Strength**: Handles messy, ambiguous, novel text.
- **Weakness**: Hallucinates; produces "vibes" not facts; anisotropy collapses cosine scores into a narrow band.

### 1.2. Layer 2 — Symbolic AI (Knowledge Graph)

- **Components**: HPO, Orphanet, MONDO, UMLS ontologies; RDF triple store; NetworkX MultiDiGraph.
- **Role**: Biological verification. The graph stores curated facts (disease-phenotype-gene-protein relationships) and performs hierarchical similarity (Resnik, Lin) and multi-hop pathfinding for explainability.
- **Strength**: Deterministic, explainable, biologically grounded.
- **Weakness**: Cannot ingest free text; cannot handle novel entities; limited to curated knowledge.

### 1.3. Layer 3 — Supervised AI (Neural Ranker)

- **Components**: PyTorch MLP, trained on pairwise tournament data.
- **Role**: Fine-grained ranking. The ranker resolves the "0.9 trap" by learning a non-linear decision boundary between similar candidates, using interaction features (vector difference, Hadamard products) and global cosine scores.
- **Strength**: Learns project-specific patterns; resolves ties that cosine cannot.
- **Weakness**: Requires labeled data; quadratic tournament cost.

### 1.4. Why All Three Are Required

- **Connectionist alone** hallucinates and is non-deterministic.
- **Symbolic alone** cannot ingest free-text notes.
- **Supervised alone** cannot scale to 10,000 candidates.

The combination produces a system that is **explainable** (via the Knowledge Graph paths), **deterministic** (via temperature-0 LLM, fixed weights, deterministic tournament), and **interoperable** (via standardised ontology IDs).

## 2. Fulfilment of Jury Requirements

### 2.1. Diversity

The project compared **five embedding models** (BioBERT, ClinicalBERT, PubMedBERT, SapBERT, MiniLM) in parallel, with MiniLM as the scientific control. The Mann-Whitney U-test (Chapter 10.1) verified that BioBERT significantly outperforms MiniLM ($p < 0.001$, large effect size), justifying the domain adaptation.

### 2.2. Rigor

The project proved statistical significance through the **Mann-Whitney U-test** ($p < 0.05$), with effect size (Cohen's $r$) reported alongside every $p$-value. All performance metrics include **95% bootstrap confidence intervals** (Chapter 10.3).

### 2.3. Transparency

Every diagnosis is **Explainable (XAI)** through the Knowledge Graph path. The system does not just say *"The patient has Albinism"* — it shows the doctor the biological reasoning:

> *Nystagmus (HP:0000639) → is_a → Abnormal Eye Movement (HP:0000490) → is_phenotype_of → Oculocutaneous Albinism Type 1 (Orpha:92) → associated_gene → TYR (HGNC:8047)*

The doctor can audit this path, agree or disagree, and the diagnosis is traceable to specific HPO/Orphanet/UniProt entries.

### 2.4. Reproducibility

The notebook is **fully reproducible** via seed configuration (Chapter 10.4): the same manifest produces the same results, run after run. The LLM Cleaner runs at temperature 0.0; the Neural Ranker's weights are deterministic; the ontology versions are pinned in the configuration manifest.

### 2.5. Interoperability

Every entity is a standardised ontology ID (HPO, MONDO, Orpha, HGNC, UMLS CUI). The system can ingest patient records from any hospital (via MONDO cross-mappings) and can export its diagnoses in any clinical vocabulary (ICD-10, SNOMED-CT, OMIM) via the same mappings.

## 3. Final Performance Summary

The project's final performance on the 200-note test set (with 95% bootstrap CIs):

| Metric | BioBERT (Baseline) | Project (Full Pipeline) | Improvement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Top-1 Accuracy | 0.74 [0.68, 0.80] | **0.91 [0.86, 0.95]** | +17 points |
| Top-5 Accuracy | 0.88 [0.83, 0.92] | **0.96 [0.93, 0.98]** | +8 points |
| Top-20 Accuracy | 0.95 [0.91, 0.98] | **0.98 [0.95, 0.99]** | +3 points |
| MRR | 0.81 [0.76, 0.86] | **0.93 [0.89, 0.96]** | +12 points |
| NDCG@10 | 0.79 [0.74, 0.84] | **0.92 [0.88, 0.95]** | +13 points |

The full pipeline (Connectionist + Symbolic + Supervised) outperforms the BioBERT-only baseline by 17 percentage points on Top-1 Accuracy — a substantial, statistically significant improvement.

### 3.1. Where the Improvement Comes From

- **Top-1 improvement (+17 points)**: Mostly from the Neural Ranker (Layer 3), which resolves the "0.9 trap" by learning the fine-grained boundary between similar diseases.
- **Top-5 improvement (+8 points)**: From the Knowledge Graph (Layer 2), which verifies biological plausibility and filters out cosine-similar-but-biologically-wrong candidates.
- **Top-20 improvement (+3 points)**: From the multi-embedding strategy (Layer 1), which unions the Top-20 lists of five models, improving recall.

Each layer contributes a different kind of improvement, validating the three-layer architecture.

## 4. The Architecture's Three Clinically-Mandatory Properties

### 4.1. Explainability

Every diagnosis has a verifiable biological path through the Knowledge Graph. The doctor sees not just the answer but the reasoning — the specific HPO terms, the specific graph edges, the specific evidence sources. This is what distinguishes the system from a black-box LLM.

### 4.2. Determinism

The system is deterministic end-to-end:

- LLM Cleaner: temperature 0.0.
- Embedding models: fixed weights.
- Knowledge Graph: discrete, deterministic similarity and pathfinding.
- Neural Ranker: fixed weights, deterministic tournament.
- Statistical tests: seeded.

Running the same patient note through the system twice produces the same diagnosis, the same ranking, and the same evidence path. This is the foundation of scientific reproducibility and clinical trust.

### 4.3. Interoperability

Every entity is a standardised ontology ID, and every ID can be mapped to any other clinical vocabulary via MONDO and UMLS CUIs. The system can ingest ICD-10-coded hospital records, can export diagnoses as SNOMED-CT codes, and can interoperate with any RDF-compatible medical database on the Semantic Web.

## 5. Future Work

The project's architecture is a foundation, not a final product. Five directions for future work:

### 5.1. Scaling to Production-Size Ontologies

The current Knowledge Graph contains ~50,000 edges (Orphanet + HPO + selected gene/protein annotations). Production deployment would ingest all of UniProt, all of DisGeNET, and all of PubMed's entity-relation annotations, growing the graph to millions of edges. At this scale, NetworkX would need to be replaced by a distributed graph database (GraphDB, Neo4j) and the symbolic similarity metrics would need to be approximated (Chapter 8.2).

### 5.2. Multi-Modal Input

The current pipeline processes text only. Real clinical workflows include medical images (X-rays, MRIs), genomic sequences (VCF files), and lab values. Extending the architecture to multi-modal input would require:

- **Image embeddings** (e.g., CheXNet for chest X-rays).
- **Genomic embeddings** (e.g., Enformer for DNA sequences).
- **Multi-modal fusion** in the Neural Ranker (concatenating text, image, and genomic embeddings).

### 5.3. Listwise Ranking (LambdaMART)

The current Neural Ranker is pairwise. Migrating to a listwise ranker (LambdaMART) would directly optimise NDCG and could improve Top-1 Accuracy by 1–2 percentage points (Chapter 9.1). The tradeoff is implementation complexity.

### 5.4. Active Learning for Ontology Curation

The Knowledge Graph's coverage is limited by manual curation. An active-learning loop could surface high-confidence model predictions (e.g., "Disease X is 95% likely to be associated with Phenotype Y, based on co-occurrence in 50 PubMed abstracts") for curator review, accelerating the ontology's growth.

### 5.5. Real-Time Clinical Decision Support

The current pipeline is batch-oriented (process a CSV of patient notes). Real-time clinical decision support would require:

- **Sub-second latency** per query (currently ~5 seconds).
- **Integration with the EHR** (Epic, Cerner) via FHIR APIs.
- **Regulatory compliance** (FDA SaMD, CE marking) for clinical decision support software.

## 6. Closing Statement

The **Unified Medical Knowledge Architecture** demonstrates that rare-disease diagnosis — a problem that defeats pure-LLM approaches (hallucination, non-determinism) and pure-rule-based approaches (brittleness, poor recall) — can be solved by a carefully designed hybrid pipeline that assigns each task to the paradigm best suited for it.

The architecture's success rests on three principles:

1. **Use embeddings for retrieval, not for verification.** Embeddings are good at finding the "neighbourhood" of plausible candidates; they are bad at confirming the exact diagnosis. The Knowledge Graph does the confirmation.
2. **Use the LLM for extraction, not for diagnosis.** The LLM is good at parsing free-text notes into structured phenotypes; it is bad at producing a deterministic, auditable diagnosis. The Neural Ranker + Knowledge Graph produce the diagnosis.
3. **Use supervised learning for fine-grained ranking, not for global retrieval.** The Neural Ranker learns the project-specific boundary between similar candidates; it cannot scale to 10,000 candidates. The two-stage retrieve-then-rank architecture is what makes this division of labour tractable.

The result is a system that achieves **91% Top-1 Accuracy** on rare-disease diagnosis — substantially higher than any single-model baseline — while remaining **explainable**, **deterministic**, and **interoperable**. This is the contribution of the project: not a single new model, but a principled architecture for combining existing models into a system that is greater than the sum of its parts.

> *"Our architecture doesn't just 'guess' the diagnosis. It first **understands** (Embedding Retrieval), then **verifies** (Graph Fact-Check), then **decides** (Neural Ranking) using a multi-layered, scientifically rigorous pipeline. This is why it achieves state-of-the-art precision for rare-disease diagnostics."*
