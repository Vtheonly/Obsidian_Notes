# 02.1. Self-Attention and the QKV Math

The **Transformer** is the engine inside BERT, BioBERT, PubMedBERT, and every LLM in this project. Its central mechanism is **self-attention**: each token in a sequence looks at every other token, decides which ones are relevant, and rebuilds its own vector as a weighted average of theirs. The original vault presented only the high-level formula `softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V` and treated Q, K, V as abstract concepts. This is insufficient — without the **projection matrices $W^Q, W^K, W^V$ and their exact dimensions**, the reader cannot understand multi-head attention, cannot compute the layer's parameter count or FLOP cost, and cannot see why the $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling is mathematically necessary rather than arbitrary. This note fills every one of those gaps.

The chapter proceeds in four layers of depth: (1) the intuition, (2) the full matrix algebra including projections, (3) the $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling derivation, (4) multi-head attention with the $W^O$ output projection, and (5) how padding tokens are masked via $-\infty$ substitution in the softmax.

---

## 1. The Intuition: Query, Key, Value

Imagine you are the word *Albinism* in a clinical sentence. To compute your contextual meaning, you need to know which other words in the sentence are relevant to you. The attention mechanism implements this as a database lookup:

- **Query (Q)**: What you are looking for. *"I am a clinical noun; which other tokens carry phenotype-related information?"*
- **Key (K)**: What each other token offers. *"I am also a clinical noun,"* or *"I am a stopword,"* or *"I am a gene symbol."*
- **Value (V)**: What each other token actually contributes if it is selected. This is the token's content vector.

The Query–Key dot product produces a relevance score for every (self, other) pair; softmax normalises these into weights; the weighted sum of Values produces the new contextual vector. This is the entire mechanism.

---

## 2. The Full Matrix Algebra (Including Projections)

### 2.1. Input

Let the input to an attention layer be a matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_{\text{model}}}$, where $N$ is the sequence length (after tokenization + padding, typically 512 for BERT) and $d_{\text{model}} = 768$ for BERT-Base.

### 2.2. Projection Matrices

The Query, Key, and Value matrices are **not** the input itself. They are produced by multiplying the input by three learned projection matrices:

$$Q = X W^Q, \qquad K = X W^K, \qquad V = X W^V$$

where:

- $W^Q, W^K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}$
- $W^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_v}$

For BERT-Base, multi-head attention splits the 768-dimensional model space across 12 heads, so each head operates with $d_k = d_v = d_{\text{model}} / h = 768 / 12 = 64$. The total Q, K, V tensors therefore have shape $N \times 64$ per head, or $N \times 768$ when all heads are concatenated.

### 2.3. Scaled Dot-Product Attention

The attention output for a single head is:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$

Let us dissect each operation by shape:

| Operation | Shape | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $Q$ | $(N, d_k)$ | One query vector per token |
| $K^\top$ | $(d_k, N)$ | Transpose of keys |
| $Q K^\top$ | $(N, N)$ | Pairwise relevance scores |
| $\div \sqrt{d_k}$ | $(N, N)$ | Variance stabilisation (see §3) |
| $\text{softmax}(\cdot, \text{axis}=-1)$ | $(N, N)$ | Per-row probability distribution |
| $V$ | $(N, d_v)$ | One value vector per token |
| $\text{softmax}(\cdot) V$ | $(N, d_v)$ | Weighted sum of values, one per query token |

The output is a new $N \times d_v$ matrix in which each row is a contextualised token vector — the original token's content blended with the relevant context according to learned attention weights.

### 2.4. Why Projections Are Not Optional

Without the projections, the layer would be forced to use $Q = K = V = X$, which collapses attention into a trivial self-similarity matrix $X X^\top$. The projections are what let the model learn *asymmetric* notions of "what I'm looking for" (Q), "what I offer" (K), and "what I contribute" (V). These are three different functions of the same input, and three different learned matrices are required to compute them.

## 3. Why $\sqrt{d_k}$? The Mathematical Reason for Scaling

The original vault called $\sqrt{d_k}$ "a scaling factor to keep the numbers stable." This is true but uninformative — the reader has no idea *why* the scaling is by $\sqrt{d_k}$ specifically, nor why it matters.

### 3.1. The Variance Argument

Assume (as is standard in the analysis) that the entries of $Q$ and $K$ are independent, zero-mean, unit-variance random variables: $q_{ij}, k_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. Then each entry of the dot product $Q K^\top$ is:

$$(Q K^\top)_{ij} = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} q_{il} k_{jl}$$

The sum has $d_k$ independent zero-mean terms, each with variance $\text{Var}(q_{il} k_{jl}) = \mathbb{E}[q_{il}^2]\mathbb{E}[k_{jl}^2] = 1 \cdot 1 = 1$. Therefore:

$$\text{Var}\!\left((Q K^\top)_{ij}\right) = d_k, \qquad \text{std} = \sqrt{d_k}$$

So the magnitude of the dot-product scores grows like $\sqrt{d_k}$. For BERT-Base with $d_k = 64$, the typical score magnitude is $\sqrt{64} = 8$, and for $d_k = 768$ (single-head mode) it would be $\sqrt{768} \approx 28$.

### 3.2. The Softmax Saturation Problem

Softmax maps logits to probabilities via $p_i = e^{z_i} / \sum_j e^{z_j}$. Its gradient is $\partial p_i / \partial z_j = p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j)$. When the largest logit $z_{\max}$ is much larger than the others, $p_{\max} \to 1$ and the gradient approaches $p_i(0 - 0) = 0$ for all $i$ — this is **softmax saturation**, a manifestation of the vanishing-gradient problem.

Without scaling, dot-product scores of magnitude $\sqrt{d_k}$ feed directly into softmax, which for $d_k = 64$ already produces saturating distributions. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ pulls the variance of the scores back to 1, keeping softmax in its non-saturating regime and allowing stable gradient flow during backpropagation.

### 3.3. The Summary

$$\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} \quad\text{has variance}\quad \frac{d_k}{d_k} = 1$$

This is the entire reason for the scaling. It is not arbitrary; it is the unique constant that makes the variance of the attention logits independent of $d_k$.

## 4. Multi-Head Attention and the $W^O$ Output Projection

A single attention head learns one notion of relevance. BERT uses 12 heads in parallel, each with its own projection matrices $W^Q_h, W^K_h, W^V_h$ for $h = 1, \dots, 12$. Each head produces an output of shape $N \times d_v = N \times 64$.

### 4.1. Concatenation

The 12 head outputs are concatenated along the feature axis:

$$\text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \text{head}_2, \dots, \text{head}_{12}) W^O$$

where each $\text{head}_h = \text{Attention}(X W^Q_h, X W^K_h, X W^V_h)$ and the concatenation has shape $N \times (12 \times 64) = N \times 768$.

### 4.2. The Output Projection $W^O$

The concatenated tensor is then multiplied by a final learned matrix $W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{768 \times 768}$ to produce the layer's final output of shape $N \times 768$. This projection is what lets the model **mix information across heads** — without it, each head's contribution would remain siloed in its own 64-dimensional slice.

The original vault omitted $W^O$ entirely. This is a critical gap: $W^O$ is what distinguishes multi-head attention from "12 independent single-head attentions stacked." It is also where a substantial fraction of the layer's parameters live ($768^2 \approx 590k$ parameters per attention layer).

### 4.3. Parameter Count

For one BERT-Base attention layer:

- $W^Q, W^K, W^V$: three matrices of shape $768 \times 768$ → $3 \times 590{,}496 = 1{,}771{,}488$ parameters (note: this is the total across all 12 heads; each head's slice is $768 \times 64$).
- $W^O$: one matrix of shape $768 \times 768$ → $590{,}496$ parameters.
- **Total attention parameters per layer: $\approx 2.36$M.**

BERT-Base has 12 such layers, so attention alone accounts for $\approx 28$M of the model's 110M parameters (the rest is in the feed-forward sublayers).

### 4.4. Computational Complexity

For a sequence of length $N$ and model dimension $d$:

- **Self-attention**: $O(N^2 \cdot d)$ — the $Q K^\top$ matrix is $N \times N$, and each entry costs $O(d)$ to compute.
- **Feed-forward**: $O(N \cdot d^2)$ — each token passes through a $d \to 4d \to d$ MLP.

For BERT-Base with $N = 512$ and $d = 768$, attention dominates at $512^2 \times 768 \approx 201$M operations per layer vs. $512 \times 768^2 \times 8 \approx 2.4$B for the feed-forward. (In practice the FFN is larger because of the $4d$ hidden dimension.) This $O(N^2)$ scaling is exactly why BERT caps sequence length at 512 — doubling the sequence quadruples the attention cost.

## 5. Attention Masking: How Padding Tokens Are Zeroed Out

The original vault mentioned "the attention mask" but never explained *how* padding tokens are mathematically excluded. The mechanism is $-\infty$ substitution in the softmax.

### 5.1. The Mask

The attention mask $M$ is an $N \times N$ matrix where:

$$M_{ij} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if token } j \text{ is a real token} \\ -\infty & \text{if token } j \text{ is padding} \end{cases}$$

In practice, $-\infty$ is implemented as a large negative number like $-10^9$ (true infinities would produce NaNs in mixed-precision training).

### 5.2. Modified Softmax

The attention computation becomes:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V, M) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right) V$$

Adding $-\infty$ to the score for any padding token $j$ makes $e^{z_j} = 0$, so the softmax weight on that token is exactly 0. Padding tokens therefore contribute nothing to the weighted sum of Values, and the attention output for real tokens is computed only from other real tokens.

### 5.3. Causal Masking (Decoder-Only Models)

For decoder-only models (GPT, Gemini, LLaMA), an additional **causal mask** is applied so that token $i$ cannot attend to tokens $j > i$:

$$M_{ij}^{\text{causal}} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } j \leq i \\ -\infty & \text{if } j > i \end{cases}$$

This is what makes the model autoregressive — each token's representation depends only on the past. Encoder models like BERT do **not** use causal masking, which is why BERT can see the full sentence in both directions (bidirectional) and is preferred for embedding tasks.

## 6. Why This Matters for Rare-Disease Diagnostics

The attention mechanism is not just an architectural curiosity — it is what makes clinical NLP tractable. Consider this hypothetical patient note:

> *"The patient arrived at 9 AM, accompanied by his mother. He seemed tired but cooperative. During the examination, he exhibited FBN1 mutation markers and presented with a tall, thin habitus, long fingers, and lens dislocation."*

The diagnostic signal — `FBN1`, `tall thin habitus`, `long fingers` (arachnodactyly), `lens dislocation` (ectopia lentis) — is buried in a 50-word note full of administrative noise. A bag-of-words model would weight every word equally and dilute the signal. Self-attention, by contrast, learns to assign high attention weights from the diagnostic tokens to each other, effectively "ignoring" the mother and the arrival time. The resulting `[CLS]` vector is dominated by the Marfan-syndrome signal, which is what allows the downstream cosine similarity with `Orpha:558` to exceed 0.9.

This is also why the original vault's "Negation Heads" example matters: BERT's attention heads literally learn to detect negation structures (a "No" → its governed noun), and the resulting vector for *"Patient does not have cancer"* is geometrically far from the vector for *"Patient has cancer"* — even though the two sentences share 5 of 6 tokens. Without attention, these would be near-identical in any bag-of-words or static-embedding representation.

## 7. Softmax — The Probability Mapping

The softmax function maps an arbitrary real-valued vector $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ to a probability distribution over $K$ categories:

$$\sigma(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} e^{z_j}}$$

It has three properties essential to attention:

1. **Non-negativity**: $\sigma(\mathbf{z})_i \geq 0$ for all $i$.
2. **Normalisation**: $\sum_{i=1}^{K} \sigma(\mathbf{z})_i = 1$, so the attention weights form a proper convex combination of Values.
3. **Differentiability**: gradients flow smoothly through the exponentials, enabling end-to-end training.

In the attention layer, softmax is applied per row of the $N \times N$ score matrix, so each query token gets its own probability distribution over the keys. This is what allows different tokens to attend to different parts of the sentence simultaneously.
