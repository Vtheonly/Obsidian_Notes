# 25 — LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023)

> [!info] TL;DR
> LLaMA was Meta's open-weights LLM family (7B, 13B, 33B, 65B), trained on publicly available data only. The 65B model matched GPT-3 (175B) quality while being 2.7× smaller, and LLaMA-7B matched GPT-3 on many benchmarks despite being 25× smaller. By releasing the weights to researchers (and the subsequent leak that made them publicly available), LLaMA ignited the open-source LLM ecosystem. Every major open-weights model since (Llama 2/3/4, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek) traces its lineage to LLaMA.

## Citation

Touvron, H., Lavril, T., Izacard, G., Martinet, X., Lachaux, M.-A., Lacroix, T., Rozière, B., Goyal, N., Hambro, E., Azhar, F., et al. (2023). *LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models*. arXiv:2302.13971.

## The Problem Being Solved

By early 2023, GPT-3 (2020) and PaLM (2022) had demonstrated that large language models work, but the models were closed — accessible only via API. Researchers could not study the internals, fine-tune freely, or build on the architectures. The open-source community had access only to smaller models (GPT-2, BERT, T5) that were far behind the frontier.

Meta's question: could a smaller model, trained only on publicly available data, match the quality of the much larger closed models? The scaling laws ([[01 - Pretraining Objectives and Scaling Laws|Chinchilla]]) suggested yes — if you trained a smaller model on more data, it could match a larger model trained on less data.

LLaMA tested this hypothesis. The result: a 65B model matched GPT-3's 175B, and a 7B model was competitive on many benchmarks. The open-weights release (initially to researchers, then leaked and widely distributed) catalyzed the entire open-source LLM movement.

## The Architecture

LLaMA is a decoder-only Transformer, building on the GPT-3 architecture with several improvements adopted from subsequent research:

| Model   | Params | Layers | d_model | Heads | d_head | Context |
|---------|--------|--------|---------|-------|--------|---------|
| LLaMA-7B  | 6.7B  | 32     | 4096    | 32    | 128    | 2048    |
| LLaMA-13B | 13B   | 40     | 5120    | 40    | 128    | 2048    |
| LLaMA-33B | 32.5B | 60     | 6656    | 52    | 128    | 2048    |
| LLaMA-65B | 65.2B | 80     | 8192    | 64    | 128    | 2048    |

### Architectural Choices
- **Pre-norm** with RMSNorm (not LayerNorm) — more stable training, slightly faster.
- **RoPE** (Rotary Position Embedding) — relative positional encoding, better length generalization than absolute. See [[08 - RoPE]].
- **SwiGLU** activation in the FFN — better than GELU. See [[05 - Feed-Forward Network Deep]].
- **No bias terms** in attention and FFN projections — simpler, slightly faster, no quality loss.
- **Efficient attention** via the `xformers` library (memory-efficient attention, precursor to FlashAttention adoption).

These choices became the template for nearly all subsequent open-weights LLMs. Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral, Qwen, and DeepSeek all use variations of this recipe.

### Training Data
LLaMA was trained on **publicly available data only** — a deliberate choice to demonstrate that open data suffices for frontier quality:

| Source            | Percentage | Tokens    |
|-------------------|------------|-----------|
| CommonCrawl       | 67%        | 3.3T      |
| C4                | 15%        | 0.75T     |
| GitHub            | 4.5%       | 0.23T     |
| Wikipedia         | 4.5%       | 0.23T     |
| Books             | 4.5%       | 0.23T     |
| ArXiv             | 2.5%       | 0.12T     |
| StackExchange     | 2%         | 0.10T     |
| **Total**         | 100%       | **4.7T**  |

This was a significant scaling of training data relative to model size — Chinchilla-optimal training for a 65B model would require ~1.4T tokens; LLaMA used 4.7T (3× over-compute). This "training longer" strategy was key to matching GPT-3 with a smaller model.

## Key Results

LLaMA-65B matched or exceeded GPT-3 (175B) on most benchmarks:

| Benchmark         | GPT-3 (175B) | LLaMA-65B |
|-------------------|--------------|-----------|
| MMLU (5-shot)     | 43.9         | 63.4      |
| TriviaQA (1-shot) | 64.3         | 67.9      |
| Natural Questions | —            | 32.4      |
| HumanEval (0-shot)| —            | 23.7      |

The most striking result: LLaMA-7B matched or exceeded GPT-3 (175B) on several benchmarks despite being 25× smaller. This validated the Chinchilla scaling insight: with enough data, small models can be remarkably capable.

## The Open-Weights Impact

LLaMA was initially released under a research-only license to verified researchers. Within days, the weights leaked to 4chan and then to BitTorrent, becoming widely available. Meta did not aggressively pursue takedowns, and the open-source community embraced the model.

The leak catalyzed an explosion of open-source LLM activity:

1. **Fine-tunes**: within weeks, the community released Alpaca (Stanford), Vicuna (LMSYS), Guanaco, and dozens of other fine-tunes.
2. **LoRA adapters**: [[01 - LoRA|LoRA]] made it possible to fine-tune LLaMA on consumer GPUs. Thousands of community fine-tunes appeared.
3. **Quantization**: `llama.cpp` (Georgi Gerganov) enabled LLaMA to run on CPUs and consumer hardware. See [[08 - Ollama and llama.cpp]].
4. **Inference engines**: vLLM, TGI, and other serving frameworks added LLaMA support, making production deployment practical.

Within months, the open-source LLM ecosystem had closed much of the gap to closed-source models — a trajectory that continued through Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral, and DeepSeek.

## Why It Worked

### Public Data Suffices
LLaMA demonstrated that proprietary data is not necessary for frontier quality. Public web data, properly filtered and scaled, can train models competitive with closed-source systems. This lowered the barrier to entry — any team could replicate the recipe.

### Chinchilla Scaling
By training on 3× the Chinchilla-optimal data, LLaMA achieved better quality per parameter. This "over-training" strategy became standard — Llama 2, Llama 3, and Mistral all train well beyond Chinchilla-optimal, trading compute for smaller, faster models.

### Architectural Convergence
The LLaMA architecture (pre-norm, RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, no bias) represented the community's consensus on best practices by 2023. By open-sourcing this recipe, LLaMA standardized the open-weights LLM design. Most subsequent open models use the same components.

### Open Weights Enable Innovation
When researchers can inspect and modify model weights, they innovate faster than when models are API-only. Fine-tunes, quantizations, architectural modifications, and interpretability studies all accelerated dramatically.

## Limitations

### Research-Only License (Original)
The original LLaMA license restricted use to research, limiting commercial adoption. Llama 2 (released July 2023) addressed this with a commercial-friendly license.

### 2048-Token Context
LLaMA's 2048-token context was short by 2023 standards (GPT-3.5 had 4K, Claude had 8K). Llama 2 extended to 4K–16K; Llama 3 to 8K–128K.

### No Instruction Tuning
LLaMA was a base model — it produced text completions, not helpful responses. The community added instruction tuning (Alpaca, Vicuna) to make it usable as a chatbot.

### Training Data Quality
While publicly available, LLaMA's training data was not as carefully filtered as later models. It contained biases, toxic content, and low-quality text. Llama 2 and 3 significantly improved data quality.

## Successors

### Llama 2 (July 2023)
- 7B, 13B, 70B parameter sizes.
- Commercial-friendly license.
- 4K context (extended to 16K for chat models).
- Instruction-tuned chat variants (Llama-2-Chat) via RLHF.
- Llama 2 70B was the first open-weights model to approach GPT-3.5 quality.

### Llama 3 (April 2024)
- 8B and 70B initial release; 405B released July 2024.
- 8K context (extended to 128K for Llama 3.1).
- Improved tokenizer (128K vocabulary, up from 32K).
- Better data quality and more training data (15T tokens for Llama 3).
- Llama 3.1 405B was the first open-weights model to match GPT-4 on many benchmarks.

### Llama 4 (2025)
- MoE architecture (first Llama to use MoE).
- Multimodal (vision-language).
- **Officially Confirmed** in official announcements; full architecture details pending.

## Impact and Legacy

LLaMA's influence on the AI landscape is hard to overstate:

1. **Catalyzed the open-source LLM ecosystem**. Before LLaMA, open-source LLMs were 10× behind closed-source. After LLaMA (and its successors), the gap closed to within 2×, then to near-parity by 2024.

2. **Standardized the open-weights LLM architecture**. Pre-norm, RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, no bias — this became the default recipe. Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, and most other open models use variations.

3. **Democratized LLM research**. Academic labs could now study frontier-class models directly, leading to advances in interpretability, alignment, and efficiency that would have been impossible with API-only access.

4. **Created the commercial open-weights ecosystem**. Together with LoRA (for efficient fine-tuning) and vLLM (for efficient serving), LLaMA enabled businesses to deploy custom LLMs without depending on closed APIs. Companies like Together, Anyscale, and Replicate built on this foundation.

5. **Influenced closed-source labs**. The rapid progress of open-source models (especially Llama 3.1 405B matching GPT-4) put competitive pressure on OpenAI and Anthropic, accelerating their development cycles.

For AI engineers, LLaMA is the foundation of the open-weights LLM world. If you're using Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral, Qwen, or DeepSeek, you're using models that trace their architectural lineage to LLaMA. Understanding LLaMA clarifies *why* open-weights LLMs are designed the way they are.
