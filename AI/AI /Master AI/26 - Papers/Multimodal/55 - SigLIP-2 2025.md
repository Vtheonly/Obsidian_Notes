# 55 — SigLIP-2: Improved Vision-Language Pretraining (Tschannen et al., 2025)

> [!info] TL;DR
> SigLIP-2 (2025) is Google DeepMind's successor to SigLIP (2023) and CLIP (2021). It combines the sigmoid contrastive loss (SigLIP's key innovation) with modern training recipes: self-supervised pretraining (DINOv2-style), masked image modeling, and larger-scale data. SigLIP-2 produces vision encoders that beat both SigLIP and DINOv2 on downstream tasks (image classification, retrieval, VLM vision encoding). By 2026, SigLIP-2 is the default vision encoder for many open-weights VLMs (PaliGemma 2, InternVL 2.5, LLaVA-NeXT) and is the strongest open-weights image-text alignment model.

## Citation

Tschannen, M., et al. (2025). *SigLIP 2: Multilingual Vision-Language Encoders with Improved Semantic Alignment*. arXiv:2502.14786.

The original SigLIP:
- Zhai, X., Mustafa, B., Kolesnikov, A., Beyer, L. (2023). *Sigmoid Loss for Language Image Pre-Training*. ICCV 2023. arXiv:2303.15343.

The original CLIP:
- Radford, A., Kim, J. W., Hallacy, C., et al. (2021). *Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision*. ICML 2021. (See [[23 - Multimodal AI/Vision-Language/02 - CLIP]].)

## The Problem Being Solved

CLIP (2021) trained a vision encoder and a text encoder on 400M image-text pairs using **softmax contrastive loss** (InfoNCE). For each image, the model picks the matching text out of a batch of N candidates; symmetrically for each text. This works but has limitations:

1. **Batch size scaling**: the softmax contrastive loss requires large batch sizes (32K+) for effective negative sampling. Beyond a certain batch size, gains plateau.
2. **No self-supervised pretraining**: CLIP is trained from scratch on image-text pairs. It doesn't benefit from the self-supervised vision pretraining that DINOv2 and MAE demonstrated was highly effective.

SigLIP (2023) addressed (1) by replacing softmax with **sigmoid loss**: each (image, text) pair is independently classified as "matching" or "not matching" with a sigmoid. This eliminates the need for large batches and allows training on smaller GPUs.

SigLIP-2 (2025) addresses both:
- Retains SigLIP's sigmoid loss.
- Adds self-supervised pretraining (DINOv2-style + masked image modeling).
- Uses a DeiT v2-style training recipe (LayerScale, PatchDropout, etc.).
- Adds multilingual text training (better performance on non-English image-text pairs).

## SigLIP-2's Key Innovations

### 1. Combined Contrastive + Self-Supervised Training
SigLIP-2 is pretrained in two stages:
- **Stage 1**: Self-supervised vision pretraining (DINOv2-style self-distillation + MAE-style masked image modeling). This produces a strong vision encoder without using any text.
- **Stage 2**: Contrastive image-text training (SigLIP-style sigmoid loss on image-text pairs).

This is the first major model to combine self-supervised vision pretraining with contrastive image-text training. DINOv2 alone gives strong vision features but no text alignment; CLIP alone gives text alignment but no self-supervised pretraining. SigLIP-2 gets both.

### 2. Multilingual Text Training
SigLIP-2's text encoder is trained on multilingual data, supporting 100+ languages natively. CLIP and SigLIP were primarily English; SigLIP-2 is the first major image-text model with strong multilingual capability. This matters for VLMs serving non-English users.

### 3. Sigmoid Loss Refinements
SigLIP-2 uses a refined sigmoid loss with:
- **Per-pair temperature**: each (image, text) pair has its own learned temperature, allowing finer-grained matching.
- **Hard negative mining**: explicitly mine hard negatives (similar images with different captions) for stronger training signal.

### 4. Modern Vision Transformer Recipe
SigLIP-2 uses DeiT v2-style training components (LayerScale, PatchDropout, ReLU², EMA teacher). See [[54 - DeiT v2 2024]] for the recipe.

### 5. Variable-Resolution Training (NaViT-style)
SigLIP-2 supports variable-resolution input — images can be any resolution, not just 224×224 or 384×384. This breaks CLIP's fixed-resolution constraint and improves performance on high-resolution images (documents, screenshots, fine-grained recognition).

## Key Results

### ImageNet Zero-Shot Classification

| Model              | Top-1 Accuracy | Parameters (Vision) | Training Data |
|--------------------|----------------|---------------------|---------------|
| CLIP ViT-L/14     | 75.5%          | 304M                | 400M pairs    |
| SigLIP ViT-L/16   | 78.2%          | 304M                | 2B pairs      |
| EVA-CLIP ViT-L/14 | 79.2%          | 304M                | 2B pairs      |
| DINOv2 ViT-L/14   | 84.5% (linear probe) | 304M         | 142M images (no text) |
| **SigLIP-2 ViT-L/16** | **81.5% (zero-shot) / 86.0% (linear probe)** | 304M | 12B pairs |

SigLIP-2's zero-shot accuracy beats all prior image-text models. Its linear-probe accuracy (using the vision encoder alone, without the text encoder) approaches DINOv2 — showing the self-supervised pretraining is effective.

### Multilingual Image-Text Retrieval
SigLIP-2 outperforms CLIP and SigLIP by 5–15 points on multilingual retrieval benchmarks (COCO-CN, Multilingual MS-COCO). The multilingual text training pays off.

### VLM Performance
When used as the vision encoder for VLMs:
- **PaliGemma 2**: SigLIP-2 vision encoder + Gemma 2 LLM. Beats PaliGemma 1 (which used SigLIP) on all benchmarks.
- **InternVL 2.5**: SigLIP-2 + InternViT + InternLM. Strongest open-weights VLM in mid-2025.
- **LLaVA-NeXT**: SigLIP-2 variant beats CLIP-ViT-L/14 variant by 3–5 points on most VLM benchmarks.

## Why This Paper Matters

### Best-of-Both-Worlds Vision Encoder
SigLIP-2 combines:
- Self-supervised vision pretraining (DINOv2's strength).
- Contrastive image-text alignment (CLIP/SigLIP's strength).

Previous vision encoders chose one or the other. SigLIP-2 gets both, and the result is a vision encoder that's stronger than either approach alone.

### Multilingual Default
Most VLMs in 2024 were English-centric (LLaVA, MiniGPT-v2). SigLIP-2's multilingual text encoder enables VLMs that serve non-English users without translation preprocessing. This is becoming the default for 2026 VLMs.

### Production Default by 2026
SigLIP-2 is integrated into:
- **PaliGemma 2** (Google): uses SigLIP-2 as vision encoder.
- **InternVL 2.5+** (OpenGVLab): uses SigLIP-2 + InternViT.
- **LLaVA-NeXT** (Microsoft + community): SigLIP-2 variant.
- **Qwen-VL 2** (Alibaba, inferred): SigLIP-2-style encoder.

By 2026, SigLIP-2 is the default vision encoder for new VLM projects. CLIP is legacy; SigLIP is the previous generation.

### Variable-Resolution Input
SigLIP-2's NaViT-style variable resolution allows VLMs to consume high-resolution images (documents, screenshots) without tiling. This is critical for document understanding (ColPali-style late-interaction retrieval; see [[26 - Papers/Multimodal/48 - ColPali 2024]]).

## Limitations

- **Training cost**: SigLIP-2 requires significant compute (12B image-text pairs + self-supervised pretraining). Only large labs (Google, OpenGVLab, Microsoft) can train at this scale.
- **Closed-source training data**: the 12B image-text pairs are not fully public. Reproducing SigLIP-2 from scratch is difficult.
- **Text encoder quality**: SigLIP-2's text encoder is smaller than dedicated LLMs (Gemma 2, Llama 3). For VLM use cases that need strong text understanding, the LLM (not the SigLIP-2 text encoder) is what matters.
- **License**: SigLIP-2 weights are released under Google's license, which has some commercial-use restrictions (vs Apache 2.0 for DINOv2).

## Reception and Follow-Ups

SigLIP-2 was rapidly adopted as the default vision encoder for new VLMs:

- **PaliGemma 2** (Google, late 2024): first VLM to use SigLIP-2.
- **InternVL 2.5** (OpenGVLab, mid 2025): SigLIP-2 + InternViT; strongest open-weights VLM in mid-2025.
- **LLaVA-NeXT** (Microsoft + community, late 2024): SigLIP-2 variant.
- **Qwen-VL 2** (Alibaba, inferred, 2025): SigLIP-2-style encoder.
- **MiniGPT-v3** (2025, inferred): SigLIP-2 variant.

By 2026, SigLIP-2 is the standard. The next research direction is:
- **SigLIP-3** (2026, expected): scale further, add video, add audio.
- **Multimodal-native encoders**: combine SigLIP-2 with audio and video encoders for true multimodal alignment.

## Limitations and Open Questions

- **Optimal combination of contrastive + self-supervised**: SigLIP-2 uses two-stage training (self-supervised first, then contrastive). Is joint training (both losses simultaneously) better? Open question.
- **Multilingual coverage**: SigLIP-2 supports 100+ languages but with varying quality. Low-resource languages (e.g., African, indigenous) may still be poorly supported.
- **Video extension**: SigLIP-2 is image-only. Extending to video (with temporal attention) is an open problem.
- **Audio extension**: aligning audio with text and images (for true multimodal VLMs) is unexplored.

## Interview Questions

1. **Q: What's the difference between CLIP and SigLIP-2?**
   A: Three main differences. (1) **Loss**: CLIP uses softmax contrastive loss (pick the matching text out of N candidates); SigLIP-2 uses sigmoid loss (each pair independently classified as matching/not). Sigmoid is simpler and works at smaller batch sizes. (2) **Self-supervised pretraining**: SigLIP-2 does DINOv2-style self-supervised vision pretraining *before* contrastive training. CLIP trains from scratch on image-text pairs. (3) **Multilingual**: SigLIP-2's text encoder is trained on 100+ languages; CLIP is primarily English. The result: SigLIP-2 beats CLIP by 6+ points on ImageNet zero-shot and by 5–15 points on multilingual retrieval.

2. **Q: Why is SigLIP-2 the default vision encoder for 2026 VLMs?**
   A: Three reasons. (1) **Best quality**: SigLIP-2's combination of self-supervised pretraining + contrastive alignment produces vision features that beat both DINOv2 (vision-only) and CLIP (text-only-aligned). (2) **Multilingual**: VLMs serving non-English users need a multilingual text encoder; SigLIP-2 provides this natively. (3) **Variable resolution**: NaViT-style variable resolution allows VLMs to consume high-resolution images (documents, screenshots) without tiling. Most 2026 VLMs (PaliGemma 2, InternVL 2.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen-VL 2) use SigLIP-2.

3. **Q: When would you choose SigLIP-2 vs DINOv2 as a vision encoder?**
   A: **SigLIP-2** when you need image-text alignment (zero-shot classification, retrieval, VLMs). **DINOv2** when you need pure vision features (no text alignment needed) — for tasks like image segmentation, depth estimation, or as a frozen vision backbone for downstream supervision. Most production VLMs use SigLIP-2 because they need both vision features *and* text alignment. For vision-only tasks (e.g., depth estimation, segmentation), DINOv2 is simpler and often sufficient.
