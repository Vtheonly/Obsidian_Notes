## Why this chapter is the centerpiece of the vault

If you read only one chapter in this vault, read this one.

The Transformer is not just another architecture. It is the architecture that unified natural language processing, computer vision, speech, and protein structure prediction under a single computational primitive — **attention**. Every state-of-the-art model since 2020 — GPT, BERT, T5, ViT, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion's text encoder, AlphaFold 2, Whisper — is built on Transformers. Understanding this chapter is understanding the foundation of modern AI.

The chapter is built so that **nothing feels arbitrary.** Every component of the Transformer — the Query/Key/Value projection, the $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling, the multi-head structure, the positional encodings, the LayerNorm, the residual connections — is presented as the response to a specific problem. By the end, you should be able to look at the famous "Attention Is All You Need" architecture diagram and explain every arrow.

---

## The pedagogical arc

The chapter follows the historical and logical trajectory:

1. **The attention mechanism** (08.1) — what attention *is*, derived from the Encoder-Decoder bottleneck of Chapter 07. We start with Bahdanau attention (the 2015 version that fixed RNN translation) and generalize to dot-product attention, then scaled dot-product attention, then multi-head attention.

2. **The Transformer architecture** (08.2) — how to assemble attention into a full sequence model. We build the encoder block and decoder block piece by piece, explain positional encoding, and arrive at the full Vaswani et al. 2017 architecture.

3. **Vision Transformers** (08.3) — how an NLP architecture conquered computer vision. This is the deep dive you asked for: patch embedding, the CLS token, the inductive bias comparison with CNNs, and *why* ViTs need more data than CNNs.

4. **Encoder, Decoder, and the design space** (08.4) — the deep dive on Encoder-Decoder you asked for. Why BERT is encoder-only, why GPT is decoder-only, why T5 is encoder-decoder, and what each design is good for. The information bottleneck, latent space, and autoregressive vs parallel encoding all live here.

## How to read this chapter

The chapter is sequential — each note assumes the previous one. But the *intuition* matters more than the math. If you understand:

- Why attention is a "soft lookup" (08.1.01)
- Why Q, K, V are three different projections of the same input (08.1.02)
- Why we divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$ (08.1.03)
- Why we need multiple heads (08.1.04)
- Why self-attention kills the RNN (08.2.01)
- Why positional encoding is necessary (08.2.02)
- How a ViT turns an image into a sequence (08.3.01)
- Why ViTs need more data than CNNs (08.3.03)
- Why BERT and GPT make opposite design choices (08.4.01)

...then you understand Transformers well enough to read any modern paper in the field. The remaining detail is engineering.

## What this chapter deliberately does NOT cover

- **Efficient attention variants** (sparse, linear, flash, flash-decoding). These are important but advanced; they are mentioned in 08.2.05 as a research direction.
- **Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)** and instruction tuning. These are *training* methods for LLMs, not architectural components. They belong in a separate chapter on LLM post-training.
- **Mixture-of-experts (MoE)** and sparse models. Mentioned in passing; a full treatment is out of scope.
- **State-space models (Mamba, RWKV)** as Transformer alternatives. Mentioned in 08.4.04; the field is moving fast and a definitive treatment would be premature.

The goal is **deep understanding of the core Transformer**, not exhaustive coverage of every variant. Once you understand the core, the variants are incremental.
