## The problem with the original GAN loss

The original GAN loss is based on the **Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence** between the real distribution $p_{data}$ and the generator's distribution $p_g$. The JS divergence has a critical flaw: if the two distributions do not overlap (which is common early in training, when the generator produces garbage far from the real data), the JS divergence is **constant** (its maximum value), and its gradient is **zero**. The generator cannot learn.

This is the vanishing-gradient problem from the previous note, viewed from the distribution-distance perspective: the JS divergence provides no gradient signal when the distributions are disjoint.

---

## The Wasserstein distance

The **Wasserstein distance** (also called Earth Mover's Distance, EMD) between two distributions $p$ and $q$ measures the minimum "cost" of transforming $p$ into $q$, where cost is mass × distance moved:

$$W(p, q) = \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(p, q)} \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim \gamma}[\|x - y\|]$$

where $\Pi(p, q)$ is the set of joint distributions whose marginals are $p$ and $q$.

The intuition: imagine $p$ and $q$ as two piles of dirt. The Wasserstein distance is the minimum amount of work (dirt × distance) needed to reshape $p$ into $q$. This is why it is called the "Earth Mover's Distance."

### Why Wasserstein is better than JS

The critical property: **the Wasserstein distance is continuous and provides a meaningful gradient even when the distributions do not overlap.** If $p_g$ is far from $p_{data}$, the Wasserstein distance is large, and moving $p_g$ toward $p_{data}$ decreases it — there is a clear gradient direction. The JS divergence, by contrast, is saturated (at its maximum) and provides no direction.

This makes the Wasserstein distance much better suited for gradient-based training of generative models.

---

## WGAN: using the Wasserstein distance as the GAN loss

The WGAN (Arjovsky et al. 2017) replaces the JS-divergence-based discriminator with a **critic** that estimates the Wasserstein distance. By the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality:

$$W(p_{data}, p_g) = \sup_{\|f\|_L \leq 1} \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[f(G(z))]$$

where the supremum is over all 1-Lipschitz functions $f$. The critic learns to approximate this function; the generator minimizes the critic's estimate of the Wasserstein distance.

### The Lipschitz constraint

The critic must be 1-Lipschitz (its gradients must be bounded by 1). The original WGAN enforced this by **weight clipping** — clamping the critic's weights to a fixed range after each update. This is crude and causes optimization problems.

**WGAN-GP** (Gulrajani et al. 2017) replaces weight clipping with a **gradient penalty** — an explicit penalty on the critic's gradient norm:

$$\mathcal{L}_{GP} = \lambda \mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}}[(\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} D(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1)^2]$$

where $\hat{x}$ is a random interpolation between real and fake samples. This is smoother and more effective than weight clipping, and WGAN-GP is the standard WGAN variant.

---

## What WGAN fixes

WGAN addresses two of the three failure modes from the previous note:

1. **Vanishing gradients:** the Wasserstein distance provides non-vanishing gradients even when distributions are disjoint. The generator can always learn.
2. **Training stability:** WGAN training is much more stable than vanilla GAN training. The loss correlates with sample quality (lower WGAN loss → better samples), which is not true of the JS-based loss.

WGAN does **not** fully fix mode collapse, but it reduces it. Other techniques (minibatch discrimination, unrolled GANs) are still needed for maximum diversity.

## Modern GAN variants

### StyleGAN (NVIDIA, 2019)

StyleGAN introduces **style-based generation**: instead of feeding noise only at the input, noise is fed at every layer via adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN), controlling different aspects of the image at different scales. This produces state-of-the-art face generation and allows fine-grained control (change pose, change hair, change lighting independently). StyleGAN2 and StyleGAN3 refined the architecture further.

### BigGAN (Brock et al. 2019)

BigGAN scales GANs to BigGAN's "large scale" — 256×256 ImageNet generation with hundreds of millions of parameters. Key innovations: larger batch sizes, truncation tricks at inference, and spectral normalization. BigGAN demonstrated that GANs scale better than expected and produced the best GAN samples to date (though still surpassed by diffusion models on many benchmarks).

### CycleGAN (Zhu et al. 2017)

CycleGAN performs **unpaired image-to-image translation** — convert horses to zebras, summer to winter, paintings to photos — without paired training data. It uses two generators (A→B and B→A) and a **cycle consistency loss** (translate A→B→A should recover A). This enables style transfer and domain adaptation without paired data.

### pix2pix (Isola et al. 2017)

pix2pix performs **paired** image-to-image translation (edges → photos, day → night, sketch → photo) using a conditional GAN with paired training data. The generator is a U-Net (see [[05.5 Deep Learning for Imaging]]); the discriminator judges pairs (input, output).

---

## The current status of GANs

GANs dominated image generation from 2014 to 2021, producing the best samples available. Since 2022, **diffusion models have surpassed GANs** on most quality benchmarks (FID, CLIP score) while being more stable to train. State-of-the-art image generators (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 3, Midjourney v6) are diffusion-based, not GAN-based.

However, GANs remain relevant for:
- **Inference speed:** GANs generate in one forward pass; diffusion requires many (typically 20-1000) denoising steps. For real-time applications, GANs are still preferred.
- **Latent space manipulation:** StyleGAN's latent space is highly controllable (you can edit specific features by adjusting specific latent dimensions), which is harder with diffusion models.
- **Specific domains:** some domains (high-resolution faces, certain scientific applications) still have GAN-based state-of-the-art.

The field has not abandoned GANs — it has diversified. Modern systems often combine GANs with diffusion (e.g., use a GAN for fast inference after diffusion-based training, or use GAN discriminators to improve diffusion sample quality).

---

## What you should walk away with

1. The original GAN loss (JS divergence) provides no gradient when distributions are disjoint — causing vanishing gradients.
2. The Wasserstein distance provides a meaningful gradient even for disjoint distributions, fixing the vanishing-gradient problem.
3. WGAN replaces the discriminator with a critic that estimates the Wasserstein distance, with a Lipschitz constraint enforced via gradient penalty (WGAN-GP).
4. WGAN training is more stable and the loss correlates with sample quality.
5. Modern variants: StyleGAN (style-based control), BigGAN (large scale), CycleGAN (unpaired translation), pix2pix (paired translation).
6. Since 2022, diffusion models have surpassed GANs on quality, but GANs remain relevant for speed and latent-space control.
